# Submission physiology, injury and tap/stoppage data (appendix to SUBMISSIONS.md)

Compiled 2026-09-22 by a data sub-agent. All items sourced; [EST] = synthesized estimate,
[PRESS] = press-reported, not peer-reviewed.

## 1. Carotid ("blood") choke physiology

### Time to loss of consciousness (LOC) once bilateral carotid compression is achieved
- **Rossen, Kabat & Anderson 1943** (Arch Neurol Psychiatry 50:510-528): neck cuff at 600 mmHg in ~0.15 s,
  126 healthy men. LOC in ~6.4–6.9 s. Subjects could walk 1–2 min after. Prolonged (100 s) occlusion →
  dilated pupils, tonic/clonic movements (Smith et al. 2011, PMID 21532128).
- **Mitchell et al. 2012**, J Appl Physiol 112:396-402 (PMID 22096121): 24 police officers, transcranial
  Doppler; vascular neck restraint cut MCA velocity ~80–83%. Ocular fixation/LOC in 16/24 at
  **9.5 ± 0.4 s**; released ~2 s after fixation; full consciousness back within ~2 s more. Mechanism =
  reduced cerebral blood flow (vagal contribution minor). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22096121/
- **Stellpflug et al. 2020**, "Time to unconsciousness from sportive chokes in fully resisting highly
  trained combatants," Int J Perform Anal Sport 20(4):720-728, DOI 10.1080/24748668.2020.1780873.
  81 real choke sequences. **Mean choke-established → LOC = 9.0 s (95% CI 8.3–9.9)**; neck-only 8.9 s,
  arm-in 9.0 s (n.s.); per-type means ~6.2–10.5 s (RNC ~8.9 s, standard guillotine ~8.9 s, arm-in
  guillotine ~10.2 s).
- **Koiwai 1987**, J Forensic Sci 32:419-432 (PMID 3572335): properly applied shime-waza → LOC in
  10–20 s; no judo shime-waza deaths since 1882; ~300 mmHg on the carotid triangle suffices; airway needs
  ~6× more pressure; O2 saturation normal within 13.7 s of release. https://judoinfo.com/chokes6/
- **Rau et al. 1998** (PMID 9741603): 6 judoka, juji-jime, mean 8 s; EEG changes lasting ≤20 s after
  release; no neuropsych symptoms. **Rodriguez et al. 1991** (PMID 1806742): 7 judoka choked to
  syncope; no permanent CNS change.
- Strangulation Training Institute timeline: unconsciousness 5–10 s (6.8 s avg); anoxic seizure ~14 s;
  bladder ≥15 s; respiration ceases 62–157 s.

**Working sim numbers [EST]:** fully locked blood choke → LOC at ~9 s (range 6–13 s); after prompt
release, consciousness returns in ~2–5 s (Mitchell) to 10–20 s (Koiwai); coherent within 1–2 min.
Convulsions/staggering likely if held ≥4 s past LOC.

### Post-LOC hold duration and sequelae
- **Sasaki et al. 2022**, J Sci Med Sport (PMID 36167661): 7,426 Judo World Championship bouts
  2015–2021. Shime-waza ended 4.1% of bouts (cadet 6.0%, junior 4.4%, senior 3.0%). Of choke finishes,
  LOC in 18.9% (cadet), 14.6% (junior), **4.3% (senior)**. Of 39 LOC cases, 61.5% had convulsions or
  staggering. LOC→release 5.0 ± 3.5 s (symptomatic) vs 2.4 ± 2.0 s (asymptomatic); **≥4 s post-LOC
  predicts symptoms** (OR 6.7).
- **Frey et al. 2019**, OJSM (PMC6545656): French judo 421,670 fights; 47 choke-LOC cases (~0.11/1000).

### How often chokes cause LOC in MMA
- **Stellpflug et al. 2022**, Phys Sportsmed (PMID 33347362): every fight-ending choke in UFC history —
  904 chokes = 15.5% of all outcomes, 76.2% of grappling submissions; RNC 49.1% of choke finishes;
  **11% of fight-ending chokes ended in LOC, ~89% voluntary tap**.
- **Mańka-Malara et al. 2025**, J Clin Med (PMC12610064): 2,488 UFC fights; 18.7% by submission;
  chokes 68.6% of subs (RNC 154, guillotine 89, triangle 35, arm-triangle 26); arm/hand locks 27.1%
  (armbar 63, kimura 21).

### Cumulative / long-term
- Stellpflug 2020 survey (PMID 32271638): 4,307 grapplers; 27.8% choked unconscious at least once;
  0.05% ongoing symptoms. Stellpflug 2024/2025: no King-Devick difference; no carotid IMT difference.
- Cervical artery injury case series: Stellpflug 2022, J Emerg Med (PMID 35934648): 10 cases (5 carotid
  dissections, 3 vertebral, 2 strokes); Demartini 2017; Martinet 2025 (RNC → ICA dissection, delayed
  stroke).

### Air choke vs blood choke; guillotine
- Occluding carotids/jugulars ≈ 3.4 N/cm²; trachea ≈ 22 N/cm² (~6×). Air chokes take tens of seconds to
  minutes, cause pain/air hunger, risk laryngeal/hyoid fracture.
- Singerman et al. 2026, OTO Open (PMID 42256725): 160 BJJ/MMA; 88% laryngopharyngeal symptoms after
  chokes; 15% permanent voice change; 8/18 who sought care had hyolaryngeal fracture.
- Guillotine = air / blood / mixed by placement, plus cervical flexion strain. Figure-four RNC = cleaner
  blood choke than clasped-hands. Kochhar 2005, BJSM (PMID 15976168): guillotine/neck-manipulation forces
  of the same order as whiplash.

## 2. Joint-lock injury data
Anatomical review: **Hasegawa et al. 2026**, Hawaii J Health Soc Welf (PMC13233104).

- **Armbar**: UCL, common flexor tendon, olecranon impaction, anterior capsule. **Almeida 2017**
  (PMC5608741): 5 elite BJJ armbar injuries — UCL rupture 100%, flexor tendon rupture 100%, bone contusion
  60%, no fracture/dislocation. **Scoggin 2014** (PMC4555620): 5,022 match exposures, 46 injuries
  (9.2/1000); armbar caused 10/14 elbow injuries; **only 1 choke injury in 5,022 exposures** (rapid
  tapping). **Fares 2026** (PMID 42484431): 2,859 UFC PPV fights; 523 subs (18.3%); upper-limb joint subs
  18.5% of subs, armbar 67% of those; women 3.03× more likely to lose by upper-limb sub; serious injury in
  ~7 of 80 filmed (~9%), avg 10.3 months to return; time-to-submission increasing over the years.
- **Kimura**: posterior-inferior GH ligament, posterior capsule, external rotators, labrum; humeral spiral
  fractures anecdotal. Hinz 2021: second-most injurious submission (31 injuries). Mir vs Nogueira UFC 140:
  refused tap → humerus fracture, technical submission 3:38 R1.
- **Americana**: anterior labrum/capsule, Bankart/Hill-Sachs; no series.
- **Heel hook**: outside (internal tibial rotation) → ACL, PCL, PMC/MCL, medial meniscus; inside (external
  rotation) → posterolateral corner. Minimal nociceptive warning; failure can precede pain. **Baker 2010**
  (PMID 19629437): MMA fighter ACL + MCL rupture. **Piekarski 2026**, Sports Health (PMID 41549501): IBJJF
  heel-hook-legal divisions **knee injury 26.5/1000 matches vs 2.2/1000 unexposed, RR 12.0**.
  **Hinz 2021** (PMC8721390): 1,140 athletes; 23.4% of injuries from an opponent's submission: armbar 55
  (22.4%), kimura 31 (12.6%), heel hook 27 (11%), triangle 19, toe hold 14, straight ankle 8, RNC 8; ACL
  tears: 41% never returned to competition.
- **Kneebar**: posterior capsule (>50% of hyperextension resistance), cruciates ~25%; literature lacking.
- **Toe hold / straight ankle**: ATFL, CFL, peroneal tendons; major cause of ligamentous ankle injury.
- **Neck crank / can opener**: cervical sprain/strain; forces comparable to whiplash (Kochhar 2005).
- General: Kreiswirth 2014 (World No-Gi 2009): joint injuries 24.9/1000 AE; knee and elbow 7.5 each.
  Lystad 2014 MMA meta-analysis: 228.7 injuries/1000 AE; losers ~3× winners; KO bouts >2× sub bouts.

## 3. Tap rules, referee stoppage, refused taps
- Submission = physical tap (hand/foot, mat or opponent) or verbal. Technical submission = referee stops
  for unconsciousness or serious injury. Submission to strikes scores as TKO in UFC.
- **UFC: 11% of fight-ending chokes = LOC, 89% tap** (Stellpflug 2022). Judo seniors: 4.3% LOC.
- Documented refused-tap injuries: Mir–Sylvia UFC 48 (armbar, radius fracture, stopped 0:50 R1 by Herb
  Dean, Sylvia protested); Mir–Nogueira UFC 140 (kimura, humerus); Rousey–Tate 2012 (elbow dislocation);
  Rousey–Budd (elbow dislocation); Jones–Belfort UFC 152 (survived deep armbar, elbow ligament damage
  [PRESS]); Jones–Machida UFC 140 (standing guillotine, technical submission, released unconscious).
- Chokes produce LOC rather than injury; joint locks produce injury when the tap is late.

## 4. Time-to-tap once fully locked [no direct study]
- Blood chokes: locked → LOC at 9.0 s mean; 89% of UFC choke finishes are taps, so the tap occurs inside
  that window. **[EST] Typical tap at ~3–7 s after full lock**; partial chokes/hand-fighting extend the
  sequence well beyond.
- Post-LOC: release within ~2.4 s (no sequelae) vs ~5 s (convulsions); ≥4 s = symptom threshold.
  Consciousness returns ~2 s after prompt release, 10–20 s in judo descriptions.
- Armbar/kimura: pain precedes structural failure, so taps are near-immediate once extension is achieved;
  refused taps → fracture/dislocation within ~1–3 s of full extension [EST from footage].
- Heel hook: essentially zero warning; tap at the moment of rotation. 12× knee-injury risk when legal.
- Fares 2026: time from grappling initiation to a completed upper-limb submission has increased over UFC
  history (defenders hold out longer).
