# Literature Review (Phase 1 synthesis): evidence base for the combat-sports bout simulator

Synthesised 2026-09-22 from `research/LIT_A_performance_biomech_physio_injury.md`, `research/LIT_B_anthropometrics_predictors_expertise_tactics.md`, `research/LIT_C_animation_ai_rendering_assets.md`, plus the citation appendices of `research/DAMAGE_PHYSIOLOGY.md` and `research/SUBMISSION_PHYSIOLOGY_DATA.md`. Organised by theme, not by source file. Full citations, DOIs/URLs and "used for" notes are in `research/BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`.

**Provenance flags (carried verbatim from the inputs):**
- **[S]** number read from the cited abstract/full text during the review.
- **[S-mem]** number attributed to a real paper but quoted from memory; not re-verified.
- **ESTIMATE / [EST]** a calibrated design guess informed by the evidence; no source gives the number.
- **[PRESS]** press-reported, not peer-reviewed.
- **unverified / snippet only** the page could not be fetched; the number comes from a search summary.
- Every "Sim implication" number in the source files is a *proposal*, not a finding; where the review below converts a finding into a rule, the rule is marked (rule) and the underlying number keeps its flag.

**Notation:** AE = athlete-exposure (one fighter in one bout); E:P = effort:pause; HIA/LIA = high-/low-intensity activity; [La] = blood lactate (mmol/L); RA = rotational (angular) head acceleration (rad/s²); PLA = peak linear acceleration (g); KD = knockdown; TD = takedown; pp = percentage points.

## 1. Time-motion and performance structure of fights

### What the evidence agrees on
- **All combat sports are intermittent; MMA is the slowest striking sport.** MMA high-intensity effort : (low-intensity + pause) = **1:2 to 1:4** in 52 regional athletes (del Vecchio 2011) and **HI:LI 1:2, E:P 1:3** in 13 sparring athletes (Coswig 2016). Kickboxing sits at fighting:non-fighting ~1:1 (Ouergui 2014, 45 World Championship bouts) to E:P 1:1.5 overall, HI:pause ~1:6 (Slimani 2017 IJSPP, 36 bouts; same numbers cited as "Ouergui 2017, PMID 27197115" in DAMAGE_PHYSIOLOGY — attribution discrepancy unresolved). Elite amateur boxing is near-continuous: activity:rest **18:1 elite vs 9:1 novice**, novice rounds 16:1 → 8:1 → 6:1 (Slimani 2017 JSCR review). Karate point fighting: activity 10.0 s / rest 16.2 s, HI actions 1.5 ± 0.3 s, HI:rest 1:11, 14 ± 6 HI actions per match (Chaabène 2014). Grappling: judo combat sequences median 23.9–28.5 s with 4.0–8.8 s pauses (Sterkowicz-Przybycień 2017, 1,411 matches), E:P 2.5:1–3.4:1 (2016) and 2.8:1–3:1 (2020) (Barreto 2022, 1,332 combats), ~7 (IQR 3–12) combat/pause cycles per match (Miarka 2012, 1,811 matches); Greco-Roman wrestling work 37.2 s / rest 13.8 s, match 427 s (324–535), work 317 s, rest 110 s (Nilsson 2002, 94 matches); BJJ E:P **6:1**, HI actions ~4 s, LI:HI 8:1 (Andreato 2013), E:P unchanged across 4 × 10-min matches (Andreato 2015).
- **Most MMA round time is low-intensity standing.** Standing LI time per 5-min round: R1 median 2:33.5 (IQR 1:20–3:56), R2 2:37 (1:24–3:59), R3 2:07 (1:06–3:39) (Miarka 2016, 645 paired pro rounds). Standing HI ("exchange") time in ending rounds: 7.4 ± 9.2 s (R1), 9.7 ± 18.0 s (R2), 17.7 ± 29.1 s (R3); standing LI in ending rounds 91.5 ± 71.4 / 93.4 ± 67.5 / 143.2 ± 87.4 s (Miarka 2018, 1,564 rounds). Bouts that end in R1 or R2 have *less* standing preparatory time (95.6 ± 62.9 s; 93.6 ± 67.9 s) than the R2/R3 of bouts that go three rounds (160.5 ± 87.4 s; 144.0 ± 88.5 s); ground combat activity 23.4 ± 45.5 / 25.3 ± 41.9 s (early finishes) vs 50.4 ± 69.9 / 52.9 ± 74.2 s (R3-ending bouts); standing combat activity did not differ (Antoniettô 2023). Female KO/TKO fights: standing preparatory 1:51–2:27, standing fighting **15–19 s**, ground fighting 47–52 s per round (Miarka 2021, 174 rounds). By division: keeping-distance standing time lowest at featherweight (131.4 s) and bantamweight (127.9 s); clinch-without-attack highest at flyweight (11.4 s) and welterweight (12.6 s) (Miarka 2017, 2,814 UFC rounds).
- **Per-round action budgets (MMA).** Younger UFC athletes: total strikes landed median **22 (13–34)**, attempted **41 (24.5–62)**, head strikes attempted 19 (9–34.5), body strikes landed 1 (0–4), takedowns attempted 1 (0–2), standing combat 2:10, LI time 2:11; ten years older: 18 (10–31.7) landed, 35 attempted, 16.5 head attempts, 1:56 / 1:56 (Dos Santos 2019, 845 rounds, 45 athletes). Female ending rounds: strikes attempted 24.5 (R1 finish) / 61.5 (R2) / 57.5 (R3); head strikes attempted 12/48/36; landed total 15.5/36.5/30; takedowns 1.1 ± 1.3 attempted, 0.4 ± 0.7 landed per round (Miarka 2021). Distance head strikes landed highest at welterweight (7 ± 8 landed / 24 ± 22 attempted per round); clinch head strikes highest at heavyweight (3 ± 7 / 4 ± 9); ground head strikes highest at bantamweight (8 ± 10 / 10 ± 13) (Miarka 2017). Head strikes received per athlete: **6.30 total / 2.41 significant per minute** (women 7.73/2.95, men 6.20/2.37) (Mańka-Malara 2022, 2,488 UFC fights).
- **Takedowns.** Modern men's UFC takedown density is low and declining: −0.0043 takedowns/min/yr (r = −0.75; 2002–2025 subset −0.0020/min/yr); median density 0 in most men's divisions (flyweight 0.067/min); winners median 0.067/min vs losers 0.000; **TD success 50% vs 20%**; control time per successful TD **99.8 s vs 78.9 s**; weight-class differences tiny (ε² 0.005–0.007); women's trend n.s. (Ma 2026, 8,461 bouts / 16,922 fighter-bouts). Winners' takedown accuracy 50.9 ± 32.5% vs losers 29.0 ± 34.7% (d = 0.62) (James 2017, 234 bouts).
- **Boxing.** Elite amateur: ~1.4 actions/s, ~20 punches/min, ~2.5 defensive movements/min, ~47 vertical hip movements/min, rounds ~200 s effective (Davis 2015, 29 Olympic bouts); post-2013 rules: ~1.55 actions/s, ~21 punches, ~3.6 defensive movements, ~56 hip movements per minute (Davis 2018, 50 World Championship bouts). Regional/national amateur: ~25 punches and ~10 defences per minute, ~105 attacks and ~183 punches per bout (Thomson & Lamb 2016, 42 bouts). Novice 3 × 2 min: total head punches 121.3 (winners) vs 96.0 (losers) (Davis 2013). Pro boxing landed punches per round (both boxers): 56.8 (junior lightweight) vs 73.8 (heavyweight); cumulative landed force 55.7 kN vs 82.1 kN per round (Pierce 2006, 6 bouts, 1,675 punches). Sparring head-contact rate: 5,168 video-identified contacts in 115 two-minute rounds (~45/round) (Le Flao 2024) — far above competition's ~6/min.
- **Kickboxing / Muay Thai / K-1.** HIA 2.2 ± 1.2 s; LIA 2.3 ± 0.8 s; pauses 5.4 ± 4.3 s; 3.4 ± 1.2 s between HIAs; HIA count/round 27.1 → 25.1 → 24.9; pause time/round 12.8 → 22.3 → 24.6 s; no weight-class effect (Ouergui 2014). E:P by round 1:1 → 1:1.5 → ~1:2; heavyweights 1:1; technique mix males **63.4% upper limb / 36.6% lower limb; 56.9% head / 43.1% body-leg targets** (Slimani 2017 IJSPP). K-1 official bouts: **86 ± 23 blows per fight, 32.7/32.6/34.7% by round**; punches ≈ kicks > knees (Cimadoro 2018, n = 8).
- **Grappling state structure.** Elite no-gi (2019 ADCC, 93 matches): most actions < 1 per competitor per match except submission attempts (1.03/match); guard pass → guard pass 0.30; takedown attempt → submission attempt 0.15; **back-take → submission 0.45** (Lamas 2024). Hybrid-rules 10-min bouts: only 19.4% went the distance, **68.8% ended within 5 min** (Pinto 2020, 170 fights).
- **Finish-time distributions.** Mean time to head-trauma KO 6:06 (range 5 s – 24:10); title fights 8:57 vs 5:30 non-title (Mańka-Malara 2022). KO/TKO ≈ 60% of finish rounds; submissions > 30% of finishes in R1–R2 (Miarka 2018). UFC fights ≥ 3 rounds 59.4%; decision 50.5%, KO 31.2% (Jones 2023, 1,462 fighters).

### Where it conflicts
- **Round-by-round MMA pacing.** del Vecchio 2011: only one round effect (ground LI longer in R2 than R3). Miarka 2016: LI standing lowest in R3 (~17% less than R1–R2). Antoniettô 2023: standing combat activity does not differ by round; ground activity doubles in 3-round bouts. Miarka 2021: non-ending female rounds statistically similar. Kickboxing: HIA count falls ~8% and pauses double R1 → R3 (Ouergui 2014); K-1 blows evenly spread 33/33/35% (Cimadoro 2018).
- **Sparring vs competition rates.** Sensor/sparring head-contact counts (~45/round; sensor sensitivity 35–86% by device, Le Flao 2024) disagree 2–3× with video-coded competition exposure (6.3/min, Mańka-Malara 2022).
- **Clinch as a phase** is coded in only one MMA time-motion dataset (Miarka 2017: 10–13 s clinch-without-attack per round); the rest code standing vs ground only.

### Strength of evidence
Large video-coded pro datasets (645–2,814 UFC rounds; 8,461 bouts; 2,488 fights) for MMA phase time and volumes — strong for medians, but rounds are treated independently and coding schemes differ. Kickboxing/boxing elite data are World Championship/Olympic level with n = 29–72 boxers. Judo/wrestling from Olympic qualifiers and Worlds (n = 94–1,811 matches). BJJ from regional competition (n = 35) and 10-athlete simulations. MMA sparring physiology n = 8–20.

> **Conclusions for the simulator**
> 1. (rule) Model every discipline as an HIA / LIA / pause state machine (Franchini 2020). MMA default HI:(LI+pause) = **1:3** (range 1:2–1:4); kickboxing 1:1 → 1:2 across rounds; elite boxing activity:rest 18:1 (pause fraction ≈ 5%), novice 9:1 (≈ 10%, doubling per round 6% → 11% → 14%); judo E:P ~3:1 with ~26 s combat + ~6 s reset cycles, ~7 cycles per match; wrestling/clinch work bouts ~37 s / ~14 s reset; BJJ E:P 6:1 with ~4 s bursts every ~30 s.
> 2. (rule) MMA 5-min round budget per fighter: neutral standing ≈ **130–157 s** (IQR ± ~75 s), shaved ~20 s in R3; standing exchange time **7–18 s** total, right-skewed (SD ≥ mean); ground activity ~25 s (early-finish bouts) to ~50 s (three-round bouts); clinch-without-attack ~10–13 s. Striking exchange = ~2 s burst, inter-exchange gap ~3.4 s, ~25–27 exchanges per 3-min kickboxing round.
> 3. (rule) Per fighter per MMA round: ~40 strike attempts (IQR 25–62), ~22 landed (≈ 54%), ~half to the head; body-strike attempts have low value; ~1 TD attempt (IQR 0–2) at base success ~35% (winner 50% / loser 20%); a successful TD yields ~80–100 s of control (exponential, mean ~90 s, truncated by round end). Do not scale TD rate by weight class; era parameter: TD density declines ~0.002–0.004/min/yr.
> 4. (rule) Head-strike exposure per fighter ≈ 6 landed/min, ~2.4 "significant"/min.
> 5. (rule) Boxing output: elite 20–21 punches/min, 1.4–1.55 actions/s, ~3.6 defensive actions/min; amateur ~25 punches, ~10 defences/min; pro landed punches ~57–74 per round for both boxers combined.
> 6. (rule) Kickboxing technique mix ≈ 63% punches / 37% kicks-knees, 57% head-targeted; K-1 ~29 strikes/round/fighter, evenly spread.
> 7. (rule) Finish mix (modern UFC): decision ~50%, KO/TKO ~31%, submission ~18%; KO/TKO ≈ 60% of finish rounds, submission share higher in R1–R2. Early finishes come from *less* neutral time (engagement rate), not longer exchanges. Median KO time ~6 min: KO hazard is front-loaded but persistent.
> 8. (rule) Keep per-round base rates roughly stationary and let fatigue/damage state modulate; apply an "official bout" intensity multiplier ~1.3–1.4 when calibrating from sparring data (Chaabène 2014: [La] 11.1 official vs 7.8 simulated).
> 9. (rule) Grappling transitions at elite level: submission attempts ≈ 1 per match; back-take → sub 0.45; guard-pass chain 0.30; TD attempt → sub attempt 0.15.

## 2. Strike biomechanics and impact

### What the evidence agrees on
- **Lab maximal punch force scales with skill tier and hand.** Rear straight elite / intermediate / novice **4800 ± 227 / 3722 ± 133 / 2381 ± 116 N**; lead straight **2847 ± 225 / 2283 ± 126 / 1604 ± 97 N** (Smith 2000, 7/8/8 boxers). Rear > lead for all (p < .001); lead ≈ 0.59 of rear at every tier; novice ≈ 0.50, intermediate ≈ 0.78 of elite.
- **Olympic-level dummy impacts.** Straight punch to a Hybrid III face: **force 3427 ± 811 N; hand velocity 9.14 ± 2.06 m/s; effective mass 2.9 ± 2.0 kg; jaw load 876 ± 288 N; PLA 58 ± 13 g; RA 6343 ± 1789 rad/s²; neck shear 994 ± 318 N**; force rises with weight class mainly via effective mass (Walilko 2005, 7 boxers, 18 punches). Hooks: **hand Δv 11.0 ± 3.4 m/s; force 4405 ± 2318 N; PLA 71.2 ± 32.2 g; RA 9306 ± 4485 rad/s²; neck 855 N (DAMAGE cites ± 537 N)**; boxing effective radius from head CG 65 mm vs 34 mm in football; peak brain strain in the midbrain late in the pulse; HIC lower than NFL concussions despite similar levels (Viano 2005, 11 boxers, 78 blows).
- **Punch type and level.** Lead straight: elite peak fist velocity 7.16 ± 0.48 vs junior 6.32 ± 0.42 m/s; contact velocity 5.56 vs 4.87 m/s; peak force **1508 ± 411 vs 1035 ± 220 N (21.0 vs 15.6 N/kg)**; impulse 24.7 vs 16.8 N·ms; lead-leg RFD 16.9 vs 10.3 N/ms (Liu 2022, 8 vs 8). Elite (15) vs junior (8): more force at higher velocity for cross, hook and uppercut; juniors more shoulder-dominant; cross front/rear foot GRF split ≈ 61/39% elite vs 54/46% junior (n.s.) (Dinu & Louis 2020). In 17 elite orthodox boxers the rear straight has greater trunk ROM and rotational velocity and higher peak force, peak velocity and impact velocity than the lead straight (p < .05), which completes faster (Wan & Liu 2026). Punch impact correlates with jump/squat/bench power **r = 0.67–0.85**, lower-limb power strongest; self-selected distance > fixed distance for jab impact (Loturco 2016, 15 national team). One week of optimum-power-load training: punch impact +~8%, squat power +12–14%, transfer ≈ 0.80 (Loturco 2021, n = 8). Effective mass is augmented during impact by joint stiffening ("double-peak" activation) beyond a rigid-mass model (Lenetsky 2015 review).
- **In-competition force is far below lab maxima.** Instrumented gloves in 6 pro bouts: **mean landed force 866.6–1149.2 N by bout; not correlated with body mass (r = 0.22)**. Junior lightweights: lead 1003 N, rear 964 N (no hand difference); 64.3% of punches ≤ 1000 N, 87.7% < 1500 N, 7/227 ≥ 2000 N. Heavyweights: lead 1125 N, rear 1098 N; 50.3% < 1000 N, 79.7% < 1500 N, 27/443 ≥ 2000 N (6%); distribution 500–999 N 50%, 1000–1499 N 29%, 1500–1999 N 14%, 2000–2499 N 4%, ≥ 2500 N 2%. Max punch 5358 N (cruiserweight); hardest HW punch 3554 N. Force did not decline across rounds (JLW last round 1098 vs 898 N). In all 3 decisions the boxer with more punches and more cumulative force won unanimously (Pierce 2006).
- **Kicks.** Roundhouse impact force Muay Thai **1400 ± 419 N**, karate 1211 ± 219 N, taekwondo 1547 ± 530 N (n.s.); foot velocity at impact 7.22 / 5.57 / 6.36 m/s; max foot velocity 13.2–14.7 m/s; **execution time Muay Thai 1.02 ± 0.15 s vs TKD 1.54 ± 0.52 s**; foot velocity ↔ force r = 0.66 (Gavagan & Sayers 2017, 8 per style). Front kick impact force **47% / 92% / 120% higher than roundhouse** (novice / sub-elite / elite); roundhouse max foot velocity 44–48% higher than front kick (Vagner 2023, 43 articles). Experts' roundhouse force is distance-invariant while novices' force correlates with body mass; experts faster at all three distances (Falco 2009, 31 TKD). Medallists kick harder and faster from non-preferred distances; non-medallists' timing suffers (Estevan 2011, 27 TKD). TKD turning kick on a helmeted headform: **130.1 ± 51.7 g** linear (axe kick 55.0 ± 20.1 g); rotation not measured (Fife 2013 [S]). Leg-kick mechanics: high impact linked to pivoted support leg and proximal-to-distal sequencing; shin vs instep trades tibial stress for foot/ankle trauma (Razm 2026, 23 studies, narrative).
- **Fatigue and force.** After 9 × 1-min rowing, 28 highly trained boxers showed significant force reductions for all punch types and delayed RFD for all except the jab; larger effects for crosses and hooks (Dunn 2022).
- **Gloves.** Hybrid III pendulum hooks at 27–29 J and 54–58 J: **all padding reduced linear but not rotational dosage; MMA glove + bare head gave higher rotational dosage than boxing glove + bare head at equal energy**; boxing glove + headgear best overall; FE brain strain risk persisted (Bartsch 2012, 54 impacts). Sparring peaks in 30 M + 30 F amateurs: **PLA 191 g (M) / 184 g (F); RA 17,156 / 13,113 rad/s²; HIC 1652 / 1079**; most impacts under mild-TBI thresholds; only delayed memory fell post-bout (Stojsih 2010).
- **Neck loads in grappling.** Guillotine / neck-manipulation forces are of the same order as whiplash (Kochhar 2005 [S]; Newton values not retrievable).

### Where it conflicts
- **Weight vs force.** Lab: force rises with weight class through effective mass (Walilko 2005). In-ring: mean landed force uncorrelated with body mass (r = 0.22); heavier boxers deliver more cumulative force per round (82 kN vs 56 kN) by throwing more, with similar per-punch force (Pierce 2006).
- **Fatigue vs force.** Lab decrement (Dunn 2022) and kickboxing power loss (Ouergui 2013, jump −4 to −9%, arm Wingate −9 to −11%) vs no in-ring force decline in pro boxing (Pierce 2006, n = 4 boxers analysed by round).
- **Effective mass by punch type.** A search-engine summary of an MDPI Applied Sciences 2025 paper (15(7):4008, doi:10.3390/app15074008) reported straights having higher effective mass than hooks and elite leg-drive contribution ~39% vs 16% novice — **unverified**, page not fetched.
- **Kick force values** come from pads/dummies with heterogeneous instruments (Vagner 2023); no head-kick or knee rotational accelerations have been measured.

### Strength of evidence
Force/acceleration measurements are small-n laboratory studies (7–30 subjects) on dummies or pads; only Pierce 2006 measures in competition (12 boxers). Kick data are pad-based. The glove study is a single pendulum series. Skill-tier scaling is consistent across four independent labs.

> **Conclusions for the simulator**
> 1. (rule) Per-punch force ceiling by tier: novice 0.50, intermediate 0.78, elite 1.00 of 4800 N (rear straight); lead hand 0.59 × rear; in-fight landed force is a right-skewed distribution with **median ≈ 950 N, ~2–6% of punches ≥ 2000 N, cap ≈ 5000–5500 N** (log-normal). Do not decay per-punch force with round number in the base model; put fatigue into volume, accuracy and defensive lapses, with force loss ≤ ~10% by end of R3 and ~2× larger for rotational punches (cross/hook) than the jab.
> 2. (rule) Head-acceleration mapping per landed head strike: straight ≈ 58 g / 6300 rad/s², hook ≈ 71 g / 9300 rad/s² (≈ 1.5 × straight RA) at Olympic level, scaled by (effective mass × hand velocity); effective mass 2–4 kg (≈ 3–4% body mass) for straights; weight class enters via effective mass, not velocity. Hooks/overhands get the highest KO coefficient, straights the highest accuracy, uppercuts intermediate. Use RA, not HIC, as the KO driver.
> 3. (rule) Jab ≈ 1000–1500 N (~30–45% of rear-hand max), +10–15% hit chance and ~0.5–0.6 × cross damage; elite jab ≈ 1.45 × junior; leg-drive/RFD attribute modulates force ~+40% elite vs developing; "kinetic-chain efficiency" (technique) multiplies force so a strong but untrained fighter gets less of their strength into the punch.
> 4. (rule) Roundhouse ≈ 1200–1550 N with 1.0–1.5 s execution (vs ~0.1–0.3 s punch): lower landing probability than punches at equal skill, damage comparable or higher per landed kick; Muay Thai style = fastest; front kick/teep = force/push tool (+47% novice to +120% elite over roundhouse), roundhouse = speed tool. Novices: scale kick force with body mass; experts: distance-invariant force, distance-dependent execution time. "Range comfort" penalty scaled by (1 − skill).
> 5. (rule) Impact quality = effective mass × velocity × stiffness term; a planted/committed stance multiplies effective mass; strikes thrown retreating get reduced effective mass; fighting at preferred range increases impact.
> 6. (rule) Gloves modify linear damage (cuts/fractures) more than KO risk; MMA gloves: rotational (KO) coefficient ≈ 1.1–1.2 × boxing gloves and higher cut probability.
> 7. (rule) Sample per-strike head acceleration from a heavy-tailed distribution: rare "perfect" shots reach 2–3 × concussive means (191 g / 17,156 rad/s² observed in sparring) even from lighter fighters.
> 8. (ESTIMATE) Head kicks ≈ 1.5–2 × hook RA, knees ≈ 1.5 ×, elbows ≈ hooks, hammerfists on the ground 0.6 ×, ground-and-pound straights 0.8 × (head backed by mat) — all design assumptions (DAMAGE_PHYSIOLOGY §3.1).

## 3. Physiology, energy systems, fatigue and recovery

### What the evidence agrees on
- **Lactate reaches ~15 mmol/L in striking bouts.** MMA sparring 3 × 5 min: [La] **4.0 ± 1.7 → 15.6 ± 4.8**, glucose 80 → 157 mg/dL, CK elevated at 48 h, no performance-test decrement (Coswig 2016, n = 13). MMA post-bout [La] **10.2–20.7 mmol/L, RPE 13–19** (Amtmann 2008 [S]). K-1 peak [La] 15.3 ± 1.6; simple reaction time slowed post-fight; CMJ unchanged; strikes in R1+R2 correlated with Δ[La] r = 0.76 (Cimadoro 2018). Greco-Roman Worlds [La] 14.8 (6.9–20.6), higher in long matches; RPE 13.8; forearm flexors most fatigued (53%), deltoids 17%, biceps 12% (Nilsson 2002). BJJ [La] 4.4 → 10.1; handgrip 45.9 → 40.1 kgf (R, −13%), 44.2 → 37.0 (L, −16%); RPE 15 (Andreato 2013). Novice boxing post-bout [La] 11.8 mmol/L regardless of outcome (1.8 mmol/L/min production) (Davis 2013). Karate official 11.1 vs simulated 7.8 mmol/L; RPE 14 vs 12 (Chaabène 2014). Non-contact boxing simulation gives only 2.4 → 3.3 → 4.3 mmol/L, HR peak 162 → 166 → 169 bpm (Finlay 2018): contact and opponent pressure are the metabolic multipliers (real bouts 2–4 × higher).
- **Energy-system structure.** Muay Thai simulation: energy expenditure 10.75 ± 1.58 kcal/min (9.4 METs); VO2 and HR always above anaerobic threshold; CO2-excess (glycolysis index) peaks in R1 (636 mL/min) then falls as aerobic supply rises (Crisafulli 2009, n = 10). Amateur boxing 3 × 2 min is predominantly aerobic in total energy accounting (526 kJ aerobic); PCr recovery in breaks is key (Davis 2014 [S]). PCr after a 30-s sprint: 19.7% of rest at end, 65.0% at 1.5 min, 85.5% at 6 min; half-time 56.6 ± 7.3 s; muscle pH 6.72 → 6.79 at 6 min (Bogdanis 1995 [S]). Full-contact kickboxing: more time in HR zones 4–5 (80–100% HRmax) than lower zones; HIA < LIA; full contact elicits more HIA than point fighting every round (Ouergui 2021). Official vs simulated MMA: similar glycolytic demand but elevated pre-fight glucose 6.1 ± 1.2 vs 4.4 ± 0.7 mmol/L (Coswig 2016 Asian J [S]).
- **Within-bout power decrements are modest.** Kickboxing 3 × 2 min sparring: SJ 27.9 → 25.3 cm (−9%), CMJ 29.8 → 28.5 (−4%); arm Wingate PP 5.89 → 5.26 W/kg (−11%), MP 4.51 → 4.12 (−9%); [La], HR, RPE rose each round (Ouergui 2013, n = 18). MMA simulated 3 × 5: HR, RPE, [La] high; leukocytes, cortisol, LDH, uric acid up; CMJ lower at 30 min; damage markers persist 24 h (Ghoul 2019, n = 12). Tournament wrestling (6% weight loss, 2 days): lower-body power and upper-body isometric strength fell progressively; lactate, cortisol, catecholamines up after every match; resting testosterone fell later (Kraemer 2001, n = 12). BJJ: handgrip fell after matches 3–4 but E:P, RPE, reaction time and flexibility unchanged; CMJ potentiated after match 2 (Andreato 2015, n = 10).
- **Pacing is preserved by tactical adaptation.** Pre-fatigued judoka (3 × 4 min after HIIT) changed HR, [La] and recovery perception, but **attacks, feints and RPE were preserved**; they cut grip-dispute time and increased no-contact displacement (Franchini 2019, n = 12). Pro boxers' punch force did not fall across rounds (Pierce 2006); winners' accuracy *rose* in R3 (Davis 2015, 2018).
- **Level and weight.** Strength, neuromuscular power and anaerobic variables discriminate level; aerobic power less so (James 2016, 23 studies; Andreato 2017, 58 studies / 1,496 BJJ subjects: VO2max 42–52 mL/kg/min, aerobic fitness does not discriminate; 4–6 matches per competition day; Franchini 2011: judo VO2max ~50–55 M / 40–45 F, heavyweights lower aerobic power, upper-body anaerobic capacity discriminates elite). Heavier MMA fighters start at higher HR after R1; lighter pros' HR rises more R1 → R2; RPE higher in pro than elite (R1, R3); no [La] differences; LW elite landed more offensive touches than LW pro (Folhes 2023, n = 20, simulated).
- **RPE.** RPE higher in MMA than BJJ or kickboxing; RPE–[La] r = 0.81 in striking vs 0.53 in grappling; RPE–HR training-load r = 0.52–0.95 (Slimani 2017 RPE review).
- **Mental fatigue** (45-min Stroop) slowed reaction time without lowering recognition accuracy; effects more pronounced for experts' defensive recognition (Wu 2025, 20 vs 20).
- **Environment and weight.** Un-acclimatised VO2max −6.3%/1000 m (4.6–7.5%), time-to-exhaustion −14.5%/1000 m from ~800 m (Wehrlin & Hallén 2006 [S]). A controlled ~5.3% rapid weight loss with > 24 h recovery did not impair performance indices (Connor 2022 [S]).
- **The MMA evidence base is descriptive.** Internal/external competition loads are not adequately identified; predictors of success not established (Kirk 2020, 70 articles).

### Where it conflicts
- Lab/sparring decrements (Dunn 2022; Ouergui 2013) vs preserved in-ring force (Pierce 2006) and rising winner accuracy (Davis 2018). Resolution adopted: fatigue reduces *volume, output-quality variance and decision quality*, not mean force.
- Simulated bouts (n = 8–20) likely under-estimate official-bout demand by ~30–40% (Chaabène 2014).

### Strength of evidence
Physiology is almost entirely from simulated bouts with n = 8–20; Amtmann 2008 and Nilsson 2002 are the few official-competition datasets. Energy-system constants (PCr kinetics, altitude) come from non-combat exercise physiology. Reviews (James 2016; Andreato 2017; Kirk 2020) are consistent on strength/anaerobic > aerobic for discriminating level.

> **Conclusions for the simulator**
> 1. (rule) Three-pool energy model: a fast-recharging anaerobic-alactic (PCr) pool (half-time ~57 s: a 60-s break restores roughly half the burst pool), a glycolytic pool tracking [La] 1–22 mmol/L with clearance 0.3–0.5 mmol/L/min at rest (~2 × with active recovery; effectively **no** meaningful clearance in a 60-s break; accumulates across a 15-min bout — clearance constants ESTIMATE), and an aerobic ceiling (`vo2` attribute) that sets refill and clearance rates. In-round HR 85–95% HRmax; HR drops 20–40 bpm in the first recovery minute (ESTIMATE). R1 draws heavily on the anaerobic burst pool; later rounds ride aerobic supply.
> 2. (rule) Cap fatigue-driven force loss at ~10% by end of R3 for a normally conditioned fighter (more at extreme pace); apply ~2 × the multiplier to cross/hook vs jab; fatigue raises reaction latency (~+10–15%) more than it lowers read accuracy, and compresses the expert–novice defensive gap late.
> 3. (rule) Fatigued grapplers keep attack frequency but spend longer disengaged/circling and less time in grip battles; BJJ time structure stays constant across matches while grip-dependent success (submission escape, sweep completion) degrades ~13–16% per 10-min match. Grip/forearm fatigue is a separate local channel (RPE/lactate miss it; RPE–[La] r only 0.53 in grappling).
> 4. (rule) Attribute weights for level: strength/power/anaerobic > aerobic; heavyweight grapplers −10–15% aerobic ceiling; upper-body anaerobic capacity is a key grappling attribute. Heavier fighters pay a higher early relative cost per action; lighter fighters escalate through rounds. Map RPE cost per action inversely to skill.
> 5. (rule) Reaction-time (defence) degrades with accumulated strike volume/lactate; do not model leg-power loss within a single striking bout.
> 6. (rule) Career/tournament mode: post-fight recovery ≥ 24–48 h; multi-bout days carry a few % strength/power deficit per match that does not fully recover same-day.
> 7. (rule) Altitude: aerobic refill × (1 − 0.063 × max(0, h − 500)/1000) and lactate accumulation × (1 + 0.10 × max(0, h − 500)/1000); acclimatised (≥ 2 weeks) fighters suffer half (ESTIMATE). Denver ≈ −7% aerobic, Mexico City ≈ −11%.
> 8. (ESTIMATE, DAMAGE_PHYSIOLOGY) Body damage → fatigue coupling (+4% per-action cost and −6% aerobic recovery per 10 body-structural points); "second wind" (+10% output/decision for 60 s when f falls from > 0.7 to < 0.55 over ≥ 40 s of low intensity); "adrenaline dump" for the first 150 s of R1 scaled by (1 − experience)(1 − composure) × event magnitude; per-action energy costs — all unsupported by literature, expose as tunables.

## 4. Injury, knockout and concussion mechanisms

### What the evidence agrees on
- **Head strikes decide ~one third of UFC fights.** KO **6.4/100 AE (12.7% of bouts)**, TKO-by-strikes **9.5/100 AE (19.1%)**, combined **15.9/100 AE (31.9%)**; risk factors: heavier weight class, earlier in the round, earlier round, older age; for KO also match significance and prior KO/TKO. **All KOs from direct head impact; 53.9% to the mandibular region. KO strike → stoppage 3.5 s (0–20 s) with 2.6 (0–20) additional head strikes; TKO: 18.5 (5–46) strikes in the final 30 s, 92.3% to the head** (Hutchison 2014, 844 UFC bouts). 31.6% of 2,488 UFC fights ended by head trauma (men 32.2%, women 23.1%); 88.1% of KO/TKOs involved head trauma; share by class HW ~54%, LHW ~38%, FLW ~12.5% (Mańka-Malara 2022). KO/TKO per 100 AE: MW 19.53 / LHW 20.8 / HW 26.09; share of bouts ending KO/TKO: women's SW 7.9% vs men's HW 52.1% (Follmer 2019 [S], 1,903 bouts). Australian MMA head-strike KO/TKO: male amateur 16.6, male pro 18.7, female amateur 12.6, female pro 7.4 per 100 AE; 34% of male vs 23% of female bouts; LHW/HW greater odds (Brown & Gross 2025, 143 events). Concussion-ended rounds (n = 990) vs submission-ended (627): ~90% of concussions from head trauma; **distance head strikes 13 (6–25) vs 9 (4–18)**; clinch head 1 (0–4) vs 1 (0–3); ground head 1 (0–8) vs 2 (0–10); takedowns 0 (0–1) vs 1 (0–2) (Miarka 2022). Coding of KO/TKO situations is reliable: MMA-KT 20-factor tool, mean κ 0.86 (0.59–1.0) over 125 events (Lawrence 2014). Fatal boxing bouts had more punches landed/round, power punches landed/round and power punches thrown by losers than average bouts, but not more than highly competitive bouts (Miele & Bailes 2007, vs 4,000 controls). Hybrid-rules KOs came from head punches and kicks, mostly in combinations and counter-attacks; TKO always via combinations, mostly ground-and-pound (Pinto 2020).
- **Rotational acceleration is the mechanism.** RA ranges (22 studies): boxing hooks 1,740–9,306; boxing front/jaw impacts 1,530–14,065 / 6,896–8,605; MMA hook 5,550; MMA competition 3,773 vs sparring 1,766; judo throws 276–5,081 (mostly sub-threshold); TKD turning kick 10,927, roundhouse 5,908 peak; straight punch 9,556 rad/s². **Concussive MMA impacts 7,561 vs non-injury 5,056–7,070; boxing LOC punches 11,280 vs 6,146 non-LOC**; thresholds cited: concussion ~4,500, DAI/ASDH ~10,000 rad/s²; direct head strikes ≫ throws/takedowns (Lota 2022). Football-derived curves: **CP = 1/(1 + exp(−(−10.2 + 0.0433·a + 0.000873·α − 9.2e-7·a·α)))**, concussive impacts 104 ± 30 g and 4,726 ± 1,931 rad/s² (Rowson & Duma 2013, 63,011 impacts); worked: Walilko straight → CP ≈ 0.08, Viano hook ≈ 0.60, Stojsih peak ≈ 1.0. Sub-concussive avg 1230 / concussive avg 5022 (22.3 rad/s) / **50% risk 6383 rad/s² (28.3 rad/s)** (Rowson 2012 [S], 300,977 impacts). FE reconstruction of 24 NFL collisions: 25/50/80% MTBI at ≈ 66/82/106 g, ≈ 4,600/5,900/7,900 rad/s², HIC ~136/235/333; brainstem shear tolerance 7.8 kPa (Zhang 2004 — thresholds **[S-mem]**, from paper body, not re-verified). Anticipatory neck activation and neck strength reduce head ΔV and Δω in all planes (Eckner 2014 [S]); unanticipated collisions are more severe, especially in the 50th–75th percentile band (Mihalik 2010 [S]); each 1 lb of neck strength lowers concussion odds ~5% (OR 0.95, 95% CI 0.92–0.98; Collins 2014 [S], 6,704 athletes). Repeat concussion: OR 3.0 (1.6–5.6) with 3+ prior; 91.7% of same-season repeats within 10 days (Guskiewicz 2003 [S]). Cumulative fight exposure lowers thalamic/caudate volume and processing speed (Bernick 2015 [S]). Age > 35 is the ARP "older fighter" category (ARP 2024 [S]). Boxing mortality: 339 deaths 1950–2007, 64% associated with KO, 15% TKO; more in lower weight classes; 61% collapsed in ring; decline after 1983 attributed to exposure reduction and medical oversight, not the 15 → 12 round change (Baird 2010).
- **Injury epidemiology.** Pooled MMA injury incidence **228.7/1000 AE (95% CI 110–474)**; head 66.8–78.0%; wrist/hand 6–12%; laceration 36.7–59.4%, fracture 7.4–43.3%, concussion 3.8–20.4%; **losers 3 × injuries of winners; KO/TKO bouts > 2 × injuries of submission bouts** (Lystad 2014, 6 studies). Weighted rate 246.4/1000 AE (M), 101.9 (F); pros 135.5 vs amateurs 71.0; compiled stoppages per 1000 AE: KO/TKO 173.9, submission 228.6, decision 98.2 (Thomas & Thomas 2018, 5,374 M / 108 F). Post-Unified-Rules review: injury 23.6–54.5/100 AE; concussion 14.7–16.1/100 AE (systematic review 2025, PMID 40620723 [S]). Nevada 2002–07: injury 23.6/100 fight participations; lacerations and upper limb most common; **severe concussion 15.4/1000 AE (3% of matches)**; age/weight/experience not predictive (Ngai 2008, 635 bouts). Nevada 2001–04: 28.6/100 participations; facial laceration 47.9%, hand 13.5%, nose 10.4%, eye 8.3% (Bledsoe 2006 [S]). Calgary: 23.6/100 AE; **4.1 injuries/100 min exposure; concussion 14.7/100 AE**; KO/TKO/corner/physician stoppages predicted injury (Curran-Sills 2018). Kansas/Missouri: 8.5% of participations / 5.6% of rounds; higher for pro, more rounds, KO/TKO (McClain 2014, 711 bouts). Wisconsin/Arizona: 57% of matches had ≥ 1 injury (pro 68%, amateur 51%); **losers 48% vs winners 24%**; pros more lacerations (39% vs 23%); winners more fractures (19% vs 9%, hand), losers more concussions (17% vs 2%) (Ross 2021, 503 contests). UFC craniofacial: **35/100 AE head injuries, TBI 16/100 AE**; males 37 vs females 23/100 AE; highest after KO/TKO; rising with weight (Fares 2021, 816 fighters). UFC facial trauma: **15.8% of fighters (lacerations 12.0%, fractures 3.6%)**; predictors male, heavier, more rounds, losing, non-submission outcome (Jones 2023). MMA vs boxing (Edmonton, 1,181 MMA / 550 boxers): boxers more often uninjured (59.4% vs 49.8%); MMA more contusions; boxing more LOC/eye injuries (Karpman 2016 [S]). Pro boxing Nevada: **17.1 injuries/100 boxer-matches; 3.4/100 boxer-rounds**; facial laceration 51%, hand 17%, eye 14%, nose 5%; men 3.6 vs women 1.2 per 100 boxer-rounds; losers ~2 × risk; KO losers 2 × other losers (Bledsoe 2005, 524 bouts). Victoria pro boxing 23.6/100 fights, mostly head/face lacerations; age and fight count predictive (Zazryn 2009 [S]). GB elite boxing hand/wrist competition injuries 347/1000 h (Loosemore 2017 [S]).
- **Submissions: chokes.** Bilateral carotid compression → LOC in ~6.4–6.9 s (Rossen 1943, 126 men, no DOI in source); vascular neck restraint cut MCA velocity ~80–83%, ocular fixation/LOC in 16/24 at **9.5 ± 0.4 s**, released ~2 s after fixation, full consciousness ~2 s later, mechanism = reduced cerebral blood flow (Mitchell 2012); 81 real choke sequences in resisting trained combatants: **choke-established → LOC mean 9.0 s (95% CI 8.3–9.9)**; neck-only 8.9 s, arm-in 9.0 s (n.s.); per-type 6.2–10.5 s (RNC ~8.9, standard guillotine ~8.9, arm-in guillotine ~10.2) (Stellpflug 2020); judo shime-waza LOC in 10–20 s; ~300 mmHg on the carotid triangle suffices; airway needs ~6 × more pressure; O2 saturation normal within 13.7 s of release; no judo shime-waza deaths since 1882 (Koiwai 1987); juji-jime mean 8 s to LOC, EEG changes ≤ 20 s after release (Rau 1998); no permanent CNS change after choke syncope (Rodriguez 1991). Judo Worlds 2015–21 (7,426 bouts): shime-waza ended 4.1% (cadet 6.0%, junior 4.4%, senior 3.0%); LOC in 18.9% (cadet), 14.6% (junior), **4.3% (senior)** of choke finishes; 61.5% of 39 LOC cases had convulsions/staggering; LOC → release 5.0 ± 3.5 s (symptomatic) vs 2.4 ± 2.0 s (asymptomatic); **≥ 4 s post-LOC predicts symptoms (OR 6.7)** (Sasaki 2022). French judo 421,670 fights: 47 choke-LOC (~0.11/1000) (Frey 2019). UFC: **904 fight-ending chokes = 15.5% of outcomes, 76.2% of grappling submissions; RNC 49.1% of choke finishes; 11% of fight-ending chokes ended in LOC, ~89% voluntary tap** (Stellpflug 2022). 2,488 UFC fights: 18.7% by submission; chokes 68.6% of subs (RNC 154, guillotine 89, triangle 35, arm-triangle 26); arm/hand locks 27.1% (armbar 63, kimura 21) (Mańka-Malara 2025). Grappler survey (4,307): 27.8% choked unconscious at least once; 0.05% ongoing symptoms (Stellpflug 2020 survey). Cervical artery injury series: 10 cases (5 carotid dissections, 3 vertebral, 2 strokes) (Stellpflug 2022 J Emerg Med). 160 BJJ/MMA: 88% laryngopharyngeal symptoms after chokes; 15% permanent voice change; 8/18 who sought care had hyolaryngeal fracture (Singerman 2026). Occluding carotids/jugulars ≈ 3.4 N/cm² vs trachea ≈ 22 N/cm² (~6 ×) (source: SUBMISSION_PHYSIOLOGY_DATA, no primary citation).
- **Submissions: joint locks.** Elite BJJ armbar injuries: UCL rupture 100%, flexor tendon rupture 100%, bone contusion 60%, no fracture/dislocation (Almeida 2017, n = 5). 5,022 BJJ match exposures: 46 injuries (9.2/1000); armbar caused 10/14 elbow injuries; **only 1 choke injury** (Scoggin 2014). 2,859 UFC PPV fights: **523 submissions (18.3%)**; upper-limb joint subs 18.5% of subs, armbar 67% of those; women 3.03 × more likely to lose by upper-limb sub; serious injury ~7 of 80 filmed (~9%; LIT_A quotes 7/97), avg 10.3 months to return; time-to-submission increasing over the years (Fares 2026). 1,140 athletes: 23.4% of injuries from an opponent's submission — armbar 55 (22.4%), kimura 31 (12.6%), heel hook 27 (11%), triangle 19, toe hold 14, straight ankle 8, RNC 8; ACL tears: 41% never returned (Hinz 2021). IBJJF heel-hook-legal divisions: **knee injury 26.5/1000 matches vs 2.2/1000 unexposed, RR 12.0** (Piekarski 2026). World No-Gi 2009: joint injuries 24.9/1000 AE, knee and elbow 7.5 each (Kreiswirth 2014, no DOI in source). Heel hook: minimal nociceptive warning, failure can precede pain; ACL + MCL rupture case (Baker 2010). Anatomical review of submission injuries (Hasegawa 2026). Documented refused-tap injuries: Mir–Sylvia UFC 48 (armbar, radius fracture, 0:50 R1), Mir–Nogueira UFC 140 (kimura, humerus fracture, 3:38 R1), Rousey–Tate 2012 and Rousey–Budd (elbow dislocations), Jones–Belfort UFC 152 (elbow ligament damage [PRESS]), Jones–Machida UFC 140 (standing guillotine, technical submission) — [PRESS]/fight records.

### Where it conflicts
- **Concussion thresholds.** Zhang 2004 (FE, football): 50% at ~82 g / ~5,900 rad/s²; Rowson & Duma 2013 (instrumented football): concussive mean 104 g / 4,726 rad/s²; Rowson 2012: 50% at 6,383 rad/s²; Lota 2022 quotes 4,500 rad/s² concussion threshold. None is combat-sport-specific; boxing pulses are shorter and more rotational than football (Viano 2005), so HIC-based criteria under-predict and β2 (rotation) is probably under-weighted for bare-head impacts. With the Rowson 50% point, an average clean elite straight to the jaw (6,343) sits at the 50% line and an average hook (9,306) above it: "clean" matters more than "hard".
- **Finish-type mix by era/level.** Older regional samples show submissions ≥ KO/TKO (Thomas 2018: 228.6 vs 173.9/1000 AE; Pinto 2020: submission > decision = TKO > KO); modern UFC: KO/TKO ~31%, sub ~18%, decision ~50%. Ranked-athlete career records: KO/TKO 40.2%, decision 36.8%, submission 23.0% (Ma 2025).
- **Serious-injury share of UFC submissions**: 7/97 (LIT_A) vs ~7 of 80 filmed (~9%) (SUBMISSION_PHYSIOLOGY_DATA) for the same Fares 2026 paper.
- **Injury rates** span 8.5% of participations (McClain 2014) to 57% of matches (Ross 2021) depending on definition and level.

### Strength of evidence
KO/TKO epidemiology is strong (844–2,488 UFC bouts, video-coded, κ 0.86). Concussion thresholds are extrapolated from football and dummies; the only combat-specific concussive vs non-concussive RA means are small-n (Lota 2022). Injury incidence pooled CI is wide (110–474/1000 AE). Choke-to-LOC timing is well replicated (Rossen, Mitchell, Stellpflug, Rau, Koiwai: 6–10 s). Joint-lock injury data are case series and one large survey. No study measures rocked-state duration, knockdown-to-finish conversion, cut rate by weapon, leg-kick or body-shot dose-response, or referee stoppage logic.

> **Conclusions for the simulator**
> 1. (rule) Base per-bout hazards (UFC-level, 3 rounds): P(KO) ≈ 0.13, P(TKO by strikes) ≈ 0.19; head-trauma finish share scales ~4 × from FLW (~12.5%) to HW (~54%; women's SW 7.9% vs men's HW 52.1%); women ~40–60% of men's head-KO rate; amateur ≈ pro for men (~17–19/100 AE).
> 2. (rule) KO per landed head strike = logistic in equivalent RA: `z = (alphaEq − 6383)/1800`, `pConcuss = sigmoid(z)`, `pKO = pConcuss × kKO` with kKO ≈ 0.22 (ESTIMATE); remainder split 45% rocked / 25% flash KD / 8% hurt KD (ESTIMATE). Alternative: Rowson & Duma logistic with β2 raised ~1.3–1.5 × or midpoint ~8,000–9,000 rad/s² (between non-injury and LOC means), LOC near-certain above ~11–12k. Strike → RA map: jab ~3–5k, cross ~6–7k, hook ~7–9k (elite max), head kick 6–11k; throws/takedowns ~0.3–5k (rarely concussive).
> 3. (rule) Jaw/chin hits carry ~2 × KO coefficient (54% of KOs mandibular); lever multipliers chin 1.3, temple/behind-ear 1.2, forehead 0.7, mid-face 0.8 (ESTIMATE); unseen strike × 1.35 (direction [S], magnitude ESTIMATE); neck-brace attribute × (1.15 − 0.30 × neck); moving into the strike × (1 + 0.5 × closingSpeed/3 m/s); mouth open × 1.2; prior in-bout head damage × (1 + 0.006 × structuralHead) cap 1.6; career KO history kKO × (1 + 0.25 × min(priorKOs, 4)); age kKO +4%/yr after 30, +8%/yr after 35; chin attribute shifts z by ±1.0 (≈ ±1450 rad/s²); residual dehydration `z += 3d` — all magnitudes ESTIMATE.
> 4. (rule) Post-KO window: 0–20 s (mean 3.5 s) and 0–20 (mean 2.6) follow-up head strikes before stoppage. TKO trigger: ~15–20 strikes landed in 30 s, ~90% to head, on a fighter not intelligently defending. Distance striking is the main KO channel (median 13 distance head strikes in a KO round); rounds with a takedown skew to submission finishes. KOs more likely from counters and combinations than single lead attacks; TKOs from combination follow-ups, especially ground-and-pound after a KD. Knockdown → same-round finish conversion target ~55–65% (ESTIMATE).
> 5. (rule) Cumulative damage accumulator: landed power strikes are a valid accumulator but have a high false-positive rate in competitive fights (Miele & Bailes), so referee/doctor stoppage keys on damage state and response, not counts. Damage-state durations (stunned 2–6 s; rocked 5–25 s with t½ 8 s; flash KD 1–3 s down + 5–15 s residual; hurt KD 15–40 s), thresholds (rocked ≥ 45, KD ≥ 65, KO ≥ 90 acute), structural decay (25% per break, 0% in-round; 40% permanent for the bout) are all ESTIMATE.
> 6. (rule) Injuries: P(any recorded injury | fighter-bout) ≈ 0.23 (regional lower bound 0.09; per-round ~0.056; hazard ≈ 0.04 per fighter-minute); split ~50% laceration, ~20% fracture, ~12% concussion; head 67–78%; losers × 3 (48% vs 24% per bout), KO/TKO bouts × 2; winner injuries skew to hand fractures, losers to concussion/laceration; amateur ≈ half of pro. UFC per fighter-bout: P(cut) ≈ 0.12, P(facial fracture) ≈ 0.036, head injury 0.35, TBI 0.16, rising with rounds and weight; submissions protect against facial injury. Boxing cut hazard ≈ 1.7% per boxer-round; hand injury ≈ 0.6% per boxer-round for the puncher. "Severe"/LOC concussion floor ≈ 1.5% of fighter-bouts (3% of bouts); concussion ≈ 0.15 per fighter-bout.
> 7. (rule) Cut probabilities by weapon (elbow 6%, punch to brow/orbit 1.2%, other head 0.4%, knee 3%, head clash 8%), severity growth, doctor-check triggers (severity 3; eyelid severity 2; blood in eye > 60 s; orbit swollen shut; suspected fracture), doctor stoppage share 2–4%, corner retirement 1–2%, eye-poke rate 0.6% per open-hand extension, foul recovery up to 5 min ([S-mem]) — ESTIMATE, anchored only on laceration shares and ARP practice.
> 8. (rule) Chokes: fully locked blood choke → LOC at ~9 s (range 6–13 s); typical tap 3–7 s after full lock ([EST]); 89% of UFC choke finishes are taps, 11% LOC (judo seniors 4.3%); release ≤ 2.4 s after LOC → no sequelae, ≥ 4 s → convulsions/staggering likely (61.5%); consciousness returns 2–5 s (prompt release) to 10–20 s; coherent within 1–2 min. Air chokes take tens of seconds to minutes with pain and laryngeal risk. Joint locks: pain precedes structural failure so taps are near-immediate once extension is achieved; refused tap → fracture/dislocation within ~1–3 s ([EST]); heel hooks give essentially zero warning and carry ~12 × knee-injury risk. Submission finish mix: chokes ~69–76% of subs (RNC ~half of chokes), arm locks ~18–27%; serious injury per submission finish ~7–9%; women 3 × more likely to lose by upper-limb sub; time from grappling initiation to completed sub is lengthening (era parameter).
> 9. (rule) Career mode: prior KO/TKO and 3+ concussions raise KO susceptibility (OR 3.0); exposure lowers processing speed; ARP older-fighter threshold 35; catastrophic outcomes are rare and tie to cumulative concussive load and KO endings, not weight class.
> 10. (rule) Glove/ruleset: MMA gloves raise rotational dosage and cut rates vs boxing gloves; boxing has more LOC/eye injuries and fewer contusions than MMA.

## 5. Anthropometrics, age, weight-cutting and outcomes

### What the evidence agrees on
- **Height and reach barely move MMA win probability once weight-matched.** 278 UFC bouts: anthropometric differences "for the most part have no effect on who wins"; older participants more likely to lose, and to lose via strikes; decision winners older than KO/submission winners; taller welterweights slightly favoured (anecdotal BF); greater wingspan more likely to lose by submission; taller losers more likely to lose via strikes (Kirk 2016a). 474 elite competitors: shorter competitors ranked higher at flyweight and more likely to fight for/win titles at featherweight/flyweight; weak negative stature–rank correlations at LW and LHW; mean stature:wingspan 1:1.024; "anthropometrical measurements cannot be used to predict success" (Kirk 2016b). 461 bouts: anthropometry negligible except heavyweight wingspan difference ↔ significant-strikes-landed difference (Kendall τ = 0.469, BF10 = 170; ≈ +1.6 landed significant strikes per cm of stature advantage) and lightweight knockdown difference = 0.355 + 0.028 × wingspan difference (cm) (Kirk 2018). **2,229 UFC bouts 2017–21: winners 29.8 ± 4.0 vs losers 30.7 ± 4.2 y (BF10 = 29,993; rank-biserial r = 0.18); relative age difference −0.82 ± 5.3 y (BF10 = 239,368); supported in 4/5 years and at MW, WW, LW, FW, BW (r = 0.17–0.29), null at LHW and women's divisions; stature difference 0.5 ± 8.9 cm (null supported, BF01 = 7); armspan +0.6 cm overall (BF10 = 6, r = 0.08); heavyweight only real signal (+2.2 ± 9.8 cm, BF10 = 10, r = 0.28; 198.4 vs 196.1 cm); A:S a disadvantage only in women's strawweight (1.003 vs 1.010); no variable affected method of victory (BF01 = 29–122); population stature 177.5 ± 9.5 cm, armspan 182.2 ± 11.5 cm, A:S 1.026 ± 0.028** (Kirk 2023/2024). Reach changes *which* punch ends the fight: 264 UFC KO/TKOs — hooks 51%, straights 35%, uppercuts/overhands the remainder; straight more likely than overhand OR 1.06–1.10 per cm; at +40 cm height difference straights 76% more likely than uppercuts/overhands; no effect on whether the fight is won (Barley 2025). In uncapped heavyweight boxing (BoxRec, all active pros): optimum body mass ≈ 110 kg; age and height effects "much stronger" than weight; taller/longer boxers likelier to win; heavyweights who had fought at lower classes 19.9% [5.6–31.1%] less likely to win (Fiala 2025). Pro boxing (400 contests): only age, total wins, total losses and result of the preceding contest predicted outcome; weight change, country, title status did not (Warnick 2007). Fight-level probit on UFC decisions found height the only significant non-performance variable (Collier 2012) — low weight, contradicts Kirk.
- **Age.** Direction only: younger wins slightly (r = 0.18, Kirk 2023); older fighters lose by strikes more (Kirk 2016a); older age is a KO/TKO risk factor (Hutchison 2014); late-career output declines ~−15 to −18% strike volume over a decade (22 → 18 landed, 41 → 35 attempted) with accuracy preserved (Dos Santos 2019); age predicts pro boxing outcomes (Warnick 2007; Fiala 2025).
- **Weight cutting and regain.** 1,474 CSAC fights: %weight regain **OR = 1.046 (95% CI 1.015–1.078, p = 0.004) per 1%**; median regain BW 9.72%, FLW 9.42%, atomweight 9.35%, FW 8.95%, LW 8.45%, WW 8.24%, SW 8.06%, MW 5.82%, HW/SHW smallest; max 20.4%; weight difference from opponent OR 1.000 (n.s.) (Faro 2023). 708 MMA + 1,392 boxers: regain winners vs losers MMA 8.7 ± 3.7% vs 7.9 ± 3.8%, boxing 8.0 ± 3.0% vs 6.9 ± 3.2%; **+7% odds (MMA) / +13% (boxing) per 1%**; fight-night weight differential predicted victory (international MMA, boxers) and KO/TKO (international MMA, regional boxing); not in females (Baribeau 2023). 62 winners vs 62 losers (two weigh-ins): no support for mass (BF10 = 0.667, d = 0.23) or regain (BF10 = 0.821, d = 0.23); athletes compete 1–2 divisions above weigh-in division (Kirk 2020). 75 athletes: **losers cut 10.6% vs winners 8.6% (d = 0.48, 95% CI 0.02–0.93); OR = 0.89 (0.79–1.00) per extra 1% cut**; recovered mass 6.8% vs 7.4% n.s. (Brechney 2021). 20 Bellator fighters: regain 9.47 ± 4.57%, p = 0.798 (Peacock 2025). 15 athletes after a real bout: winners more HI time (median 58 s [10–98] vs 32 s [1–60]), more kick sequences (3.5 vs 1.0), more ground-and-pound (2.5 vs 0.0) (Coswig 2019). Typical fight-week cuts 5–10% of body mass (ESTIMATE; Crighton 2016 [S: title only]); ~5.3% cut + > 24 h recovery → no decrement (Connor 2022).
- **Weight class and finish type.** Ranked UFC athletes' careers: overall KO/TKO 40.2%, decision 36.8%, submission 23.0%; males 43.8/33.4/22.9%; females decision 46.0%, KO/TKO 30.6%, sub 23.2%; heavier male divisions more KO/TKO, fewer decisions/subs (χ² 30.1, 33.2, 15.8); female outcomes independent of division (Ma 2025).
- **Stance.** Southpaws 17.4% of 1,468 MMA fighters (orthodox 80.3%); win% 64.0 ± 20.4 vs 62.6 ± 21.3 (n.s.); southpaws had more fights (22.0 vs 19.7) (Baker & Schorer 2013). Left-handers over-represented in UFC but no advantage vs right-handers (Pollet 2013); no effect of opponent handedness for hall-of-famers (χ² = 0.17, p = 0.39; GLMM B = −0.214, p = 0.34; Pollet & Riegman 2014). 10,445 male boxers (1,779 left), 1,314 female (164), 2,100 MMA (393): prevalence 17.0% / 12.5% / 18.7% vs population 12.6% (men) / 9.9% (women); P(random left-hander out-scores random right-hander) **52.4% (male boxing), 54.5% (female boxing), 53.5% (MMA)** (Richardson & Gilman 2019).

### Where it conflicts
- Weight regain positive in large commission datasets (Faro; Baribeau), null in small elite samples (Kirk 2020; Peacock) — none control for skill.
- Reach: consistent null in MMA (four Kirk datasets), positive only at heavyweight and in uncapped heavyweight boxing (Fiala) and in Collier's fight-level probit; the popular "reach-advantaged fighter wins 51.65%" figures are non-peer-reviewed and were not used.
- Southpaw: over-representation robust; small positive edge in records-based samples, null in matched-fight samples; online "10–15% more title wins" claims untraceable.
- Age curve shape: no peer-reviewed peak-age model; "peak 27–32" and "win% peaks in career year 4 (64%)" are non-peer-reviewed.

### Strength of evidence
Kirk 2023 (2,229 bouts, Bayesian, robustness checks) is the best anthropometric study; Faro (1,474) and Baribeau (2,100) are large but confounded; the null regain studies are small (n = 20–124). Fiala and Warnick are abstract-level. Ma 2025 is low-tier with survivorship bias.

> **Conclusions for the simulator**
> 1. (rule) No direct height/reach win-probability term in MMA. Reach acts on **striking output at range** (≈ +1.5% landed distance strikes per cm at heavyweight, ~0 at lighter divisions), on knockdowns at lightweight (+0.028 KD/bout per cm, cap ± 10 cm), and on **finishing-punch type** (base hook 0.51 / straight 0.35 / uppercut+overhand 0.14; shift +1 pp toward straights per cm reach advantage, cap ± 20 cm). Heavyweight only: +~0.5% win probability per cm (capped). Uncapped striking rulesets (HW boxing): +0.3–0.5% per cm (cap ± 15 cm) and a body-mass benefit plateauing at ~110 kg.
> 2. (rule) Fighter generation: stature 177.5 ± 9.5 cm, armspan 182.2 ± 11.5 cm, A:S ~ N(1.026, 0.028); southpaw prevalence 17–19% (MMA/male boxing), 12.5% (female boxing); fight-night mass ≈ division limit + 8–10% (light divisions), ~6% MW, ~3% HW; mean regain ~9.5%.
> 3. (rule) Age: logistic coefficient ≈ 0.06/yr of age gap (≈ −1.5 to −2 pp per year), null at LHW and women's divisions; older fighters get an age-dependent chin/durability penalty (more likely to be finished by strikes) and an experience/pacing term shifting outcomes toward decisions; ageing = declining output/pace (~−1.5%/yr late career) and durability with stable-to-rising accuracy and decision quality. Peak-age shape is an ASSUMPTION.
> 4. (rule) Weight: multiply pre-fight odds by ~1.05^(regain% difference), capped ± 5 pp; cut magnitude beyond ~5% costs −1 to −2% conditioning/chin per 1% (≈ OR 0.89); fight-night weight advantage +2–3% relative KO probability per kg (cap 5 kg); penalise *residual* dehydration at fight time, not the cut itself. Net effect of an aggressive cut ≈ neutral to slightly negative unless regain is efficient; must never dominate skill.
> 5. (rule) Stance: per-fight southpaw bonus ≤ +2% (e.g. +2% when opponent's southpaw exposure < N fights, else +0.5%); at elite experience ~0; implement over-representation as a selection effect.
> 6. (rule) Win-method targets by level: ranked male MMA ≈ 44% KO/TKO, 33% decision, 23% submission; KO share rising from ~30% at flyweight to 60%+ at heavyweight; women ~46% decisions.
> 7. (rule) "Form": previous-bout result carries a small confidence/damage modifier; record (wins/losses) is a valid latent-skill proxy for generation.

## 6. Win predictors and judging

### What the evidence agrees on
- **Landed volume and accuracy, especially on the ground, predict winning; attempts do not.** 234 decisive UFC bouts: moderate effects for total strikes landed/min, attempted/min, significant strikes landed/min, accuracy, significant ground strikes landed/min, offensive passes; TD accuracy 50.9 ± 32.5% vs 29.0 ± 34.7% (d = 0.62); decision tree (raw) 71.8% (sens 88.9%, spec 54.7%): **> 4 significant ground strikes landed per bout → 80.4% win (111/138); + TD accuracy > 25% → 84.9%**; rate-scaled tree 76.3% (sens 73.5%, spec 79.1%): **> 0.85 significant ground strikes landed/min → 91.5% (86/94); + > 4.19 total strikes landed/min → 96.3% (79/82)**; DFA 71.4/71.2%; attempt volume without accuracy did not contribute (James 2017). 2,831 bouts 2000–2015: RIPPER rules train 77.0% / test 75.2%; dominant rule **significant ground strikes landed ≥ 6 AND total strikes landed ≥ 81 → win** (559 TP); landing even 1 ground strike gave 77–79% win likelihood in very short or very long bouts; tactical change erratic 2000–08, stable to 2014, renewed 2015; post-2008 more distance striking (James 2019). Winners > losers in total strikes, submission attempts and positional improvements in every round (Miarka 2016). Significant strikes landed winners vs losers: HW 66.5 vs 36.5 (BF10 = 399, d = 0.84), sig ground strikes 10.3 vs 2.3 (d = 0.75); WW ground 7.5 vs 1.3 (BF10 = 3.5e6, d = 0.69), sig strikes 59 vs 24.8 (d = 0.64), TDs 1.8 vs 0.1 (d = 0.55); LW 42.4 vs 29.6 (d = 0.86), knockdowns 0.5 vs 0.1 (d = 0.49) (Kirk 2018). Winners' TD success 50% vs 20%, control 99.8 vs 78.9 s per TD (Ma 2026). In pro boxing decisions the boxer with more punches and more cumulative force won unanimously (3/3; Pierce 2006). Elite amateur boxing: winners **%Hit 33% vs 23% (ES 1.39, R1); %Air 17% vs 27% (ES −1.08, R2); total punches 76.8 vs 79.3 (n.s.)**; winners bounce, losers step; accuracy alone classifies 76.9%, + movement 84.6%, + total punches 80.8% (Dunn 2017, 19 bouts). Winners' accuracy rises R1 → R3, more straights in R1, more landed in R3, fewer air punches R2–R3 (Davis 2015, 2018).
- **Judges weight knockdowns far above everything else.** 17,105 judge scores, 5,800 rounds, 1,840 UFC fights (incl. 51 no-audience events): average marginal effects on P(judge awards round) per unit difference — **knockdowns +0.238; submission attempts +0.096; reversals +0.062; takedowns landed +0.049; home fighter with crowd +0.036; ranking 10 places higher +0.034; significant head strikes +0.021; significant body +0.015; significant leg +0.013; non-significant strikes missed +0.005; significant body missed +0.004; non-significant landed +0.003; control time +0.0009/s (≈ +0.054/min); takedowns missed ≈ −0.02 per √unit; home with no crowd ≈ 0 (−0.004)**; crowd size irrelevant, only presence; control time valued more when the controller has high submission-win probability (Holmes 2022 thesis, Table 4.2). Peer-reviewed corroboration: bias toward larger betting favourites, fighters with insurmountable leads and the previous-round winner; missed takedowns the only significant negative action; tight submissions and knockdowns most influential; no bias toward titleholders or against point deductions (Gift 2018, NV/CA 2001–12). Knockdowns and visible damage have the largest marginal effects on winning a decision (Collier 2012). Standard vs consensus scoring agree in 97.5% of 4,129 decisions; where they disagree, consensus matches fan opinion 49.0% vs 43.8%; 10-8 rounds drive divergence (Berthet 2024, preprint). Muay Thai pro (12 matches): winners threw more hooks in R3, landed them more in R4, more/more accurate round kicks in R1 and R5 (Bhumipol 2023, n = 8).
- **Home advantage.** Equally matched European championship boxers: **P(home win) = 0.57 KO, 0.66 TKO, 0.74 points** (Balmer 2005). UFC: +3.6% round-win with a live crowd; 0 without (Holmes 2022).
- **Prediction ceiling.** Markov-chain fight simulator (4,678 UFC fights, 1,680 athletes; per-fighter strike rates/accuracies by position, TD and sub accuracy, KD/KO probability, control time per TD, stand-up probability; 10,000 simulations per fight): **61.6% result accuracy on 327 held-out 2018 fights (resample SD 0.53; range 59.9–63.0%) vs bookmakers 61.2%**; ~41% disagreement with bookmakers; method-of-victory 38.8% vs 32.7%; profitable strategy; accuracy stable regardless of minimum prior fights (Holmes, McHale & Zychaluk 2023). ML on fighter stats: best 66.7% (Yan 2024, as cited by a replication). Betting market largely efficient, no favourite–longshot bias; youth and travel under-priced; women's favourites slightly under-priced (Miller & Nichols 2026).

### Where it conflicts
- The AME weight vector comes from an examined PhD thesis, not a journal; Gift 2018 and Collier 2012 agree on ordering but coefficients were not accessible.
- Collier 2012 finds height significant in decisions, contradicting Kirk.
- ML accuracy claims of 70–80% circulate in course projects and a withdrawn preprint (FightTracker, arXiv 2312.11067) and were excluded.

### Strength of evidence
Strong: James 2017 (cross-validated), James 2019 (2,831 bouts), Holmes 2023 (held-out test, peer-reviewed forecasting journal), Holmes 2022 (17,105 scores). Boxing accuracy data small (19 bouts) but tightly measured. Home-advantage boxing model peer-reviewed.

> **Conclusions for the simulator**
> 1. (rule) Architecture validation: a position-state Markov simulator with per-fighter rate/accuracy parameters matches bookmaker accuracy. Calibration target: when run on real stats, ~61–62% of favourites win; **35–40% of "better on paper" fighters lose**; method-of-victory predictability ~39%. A sim whose outcomes are > 70% predictable from ratings is under-randomised.
> 2. (rule) Round-scoring weight vector (knockdown = 1.0 reference): submission attempt 0.40; reversal 0.26; takedown 0.21; significant head strike 0.09; body 0.06; leg 0.055; control time 0.0038/s; non-significant strike 0.013; missed takedown −0.08. Add a "visible damage" state (cuts, swelling, wobble) worth ~0.3–0.5 of a knockdown per event. Three judges with independent per-round noise; scoring-method choice matters in only ~2.5% of decisions (low priority).
> 3. (rule) Judge biases: home + crowd +3.6% round-win (0 without crowd); +0.34% per ranking place (reputation); previous-round winner and pre-fight favourite +3–5% in close rounds; missed takedowns negative. Boxing rulesets: home bonus mostly inside the judging model — ≈ +7% via non-judging channels (KO 0.57), further ≈ +9% via stoppage leniency (TKO 0.66), up to +24% in points decisions.
> 4. (rule) Fight scoring rewards landed rate and accuracy, not attempts; ground strikes landed have a step-like effect (> 0.85/min → ~90% win; ≥ 6 per bout with ≥ 81 total landed → win); TD accuracy 25% is the tipping point between grappling helping and hurting; winner TD success ~51%, loser ~29%.
> 5. (rule) Boxing scoring: landed-percentage difference dominates (a 10-pp accuracy gap ≈ decisive); elite accuracy baseline 0.28–0.33, clean-miss 0.17–0.27; small judge bonus for mobile/bouncing footwork; missed ("air") punches cost fatigue with no score. Muay Thai: weight later rounds higher.
> 6. (rule) In-game market odds: unbiased across the favourite range, slightly under-weighting age and travel.

## 7. Expertise, perception, decision-making and skill acquisition

### What the evidence agrees on
- **Experts anticipate, novices react.** Meta-analysis of 27 studies / 233 datasets (fencing 16, karate 10, TKD 7, boxing 6, sanda 4, judo 3): **response accuracy SMD = 1.24 [0.80–1.68] (experts 83.3% vs non-experts 68.5%); reaction time SMD = −1.00 [−1.14 to −0.86]; fixations SMD = −2.04 [−3.32 to −0.77]; fixation duration SMD 0.64 (n.s.)**; RT by sport: karate −1.23, sanda −1.36, fencing −1.07, TKD −0.89, boxing −0.51 (p = 0.07); in-situ stimuli give the largest gaps (accuracy SMD 7.71), static the smallest; expert vs intermediate SMD 1.10; intermediate vs novice 1.37 (Zhang 2022). Karate athletes faster in choice RT to sport-specific video, **no difference in simple RT**; more correct predictions at 7-frame occlusion (Mori 2002). Boxers at −40 ms occlusion: recognition accuracy **experts 75.3% vs novices 64.3%; RT 811 vs 915 ms (offensive), 729 vs 932 ms (defensive)** (Wu 2025). Fencers: accuracy rises with later occlusion for both groups; experts better in all conditions; sound adds nothing (Allerdissen 2017, 15 vs 17). Experts fixate fewer locations, upper torso/armed hand rather than weapon/hands (Witkowski 2021; Russo & Ottoboni 2019; Martinez de Quel & Bennett 2019). Under anxiety, fixation count and locations rise, more in novices, drifting from head/chest to peripheral limbs; experts anticipate better in both conditions (Williams & Elliott 1999). Beginners' automatic responses to attack postures follow a **defence-related** pattern, experts' a **counter-attack** pattern, non-athletes neither (Ottoboni 2015). Sport-specific proactive vs reactive control differs neurologically (larger prefrontal preparatory activity ↔ fewer errors; larger motor preparation ↔ faster responses) between boxers and fencers (Bianco 2017). 8 weeks of combat training in 9–12-year-olds: earlier saccade onset latencies (Ju 2018).
- **Technique quality scales with training level** (Smith 2000; Dinu & Louis 2020; Liu 2022 — see Theme 2): elite rear-hand force 2 × novice; elite jab 1.45 × junior; elite kinetic chain less shoulder-dominant. Non-medallists' kick timing suffers at non-preferred distances; medallists do not (Estevan 2011; Falco 2009).
- **Fatigue and expertise.** Mental fatigue slows RT without lowering accuracy; effects larger for experts' defensive recognition (Wu 2025).

### Where it conflicts / is missing
- Boxing-specific RT effect is the weakest of the sports (SMD −0.51, p = 0.07).
- No combat-sport-specific quantification of beginner behaviours (flinching, eye closure, turning away, squaring up); support is indirect (Ottoboni; Williams & Elliott; startle literature).
- No head-to-head of pressure vs counter styles.

### Strength of evidence
One high-quality meta-analysis plus several small classic lab studies (n ≈ 15–40). In-situ effects are larger than lab tasks suggest.

> **Conclusions for the simulator**
> 1. (rule) Perception–action loop per fighter: cue detection → read → decision → execution, with expertise acting on the first three stages. "Read" probability by tier: **novice 0.55–0.65, intermediate 0.70–0.78, expert 0.80–0.88**; the largest step is novice → intermediate. Read accuracy rises logistically with attack elapsed time (earlier interception = lower accuracy), with a skill offset.
> 2. (rule) Simple reaction latency is similar across tiers (~200–250 ms); experts gain 50–100 ms of earlier cue pickup (choice RT ≈ 0.8 × novice), so telegraphed/slow techniques (1.0–1.5 s kicks) are far less effective against experts. AI decisions see opponent state delayed 1–2 ticks (100–200 ms).
> 3. (rule) Counter-on-read probability: novice 0.05, intermediate 0.25, expert 0.5; beginners default to cover/retreat. Feint-bite probability: novice ~0.6, expert ~0.25 (experts read torso/shoulder cues a feint does not commit).
> 4. (rule) Under pressure/anxiety (after being hurt, low composure): defensive read drops ~−15% for novices vs ~−5% for experts. Fatigue: +10–15% latency, little accuracy loss; experts lose relatively more defensive edge late.
> 5. (rule) "Initiative" (proactive vs reactive) is a per-fighter trait independent of raw reaction speed. Skill tier raises both force and hand speed; "range comfort" penalty scaled by (1 − skill).

## 8. Tactical analysis

### What the evidence agrees on
- **Winners are more accurate, not busier (striking sports).** Novice boxing: winners landed 18 ± 11 more punches; more lead-hand punches in R1 (34.2 vs 26.5); total head punches 121.3 vs 96.0; **block-and-counter combinations 2.8 vs 0.1 per bout**; combinations in R1 and R3 (44.3 vs 28.8) (Davis 2013, 32 boxers). Elite: winners more straights/lead straights in R1, more landed in R3, better thrown:landed in R3, fewer air punches R2–R3; post-2013 style = long range, straights, movement, fewer hooks (Davis 2018); winners more rear hooks landed R2–R3, fewer defensive movements (Davis 2015). Regional/national amateur: **jab most frequent but least successful (28% landed); cross, lead hook, rear hook 33–40%; foot defence 70% successful** (most effective), arm/trunk defences less; winning ↔ higher offensive frequency and success and *fewer* arm/trunk defences; national standard ↔ higher offensive and defensive frequencies; weight effects inconsistent (Thomson & Lamb 2016). Winners perform more offensive and effective actions; defensive actions must convert into counters (El-Ashker 2011, via secondary sources). Elite kickboxing: most used = straight punches, roundhouse, block/parry, foot defence; winners more hooks, foot defence, clinch, punch combinations, head/body offence, lead and rear punches, higher effectiveness (Ouergui 2013 IJPAS); winners > losers in head-targeted actions, counterattacks, jab-cross, total punches; E:P did not differ (Slimani 2017 IJSPP). Triple-punch combos, block-and-counter combos, head punches and effectiveness metrics associated with winning (Slimani 2017 JSCR review).
- **MMA winners engage more and land on the ground.** Winners ~1.8 × HI time (58 vs 32 s), more kick sequences and ground-and-pound (Coswig 2019). Ground striking is the single strongest discriminator (James 2017, 2019; Kirk 2018). Early finishes come from less neutral time (Antoniettô 2023). Landed head/body/total strikes positively associated with performance probability; attempted body strikes and submission attempts negatively (Dos Santos 2019). Higher level = more landed output at the same weight; lighter = higher pace and steeper fatigue (Folhes 2023).
- **Exchange grammar.** Olympic TKD (11,474 M / 12,980 F actions): 32 (M) / 30 (F) significant sequences; initiations ≈ 1/3 attack, 1/3 counter-attack, 1/3 defensive; most common: opening + dodge, direct attack + simultaneous counter, dodge + direct attack, indirect attack + simultaneous counter (Menescardi 2019). Hybrid rules: KO tools are head punches and kicks in combinations and counters; TKO always via combinations; finish hazard front-loaded (Pinto 2020). Elite no-gi transitions (Lamas 2024, Theme 1).
- **Division-specific phase budgets** (Miarka 2017): distance standing ~130–150 s/round; clinch-no-attack 10–13 s; distance head-strike accuracy ≈ 7/24 ≈ 0.29 (WW); ground strike accuracy ≈ 0.8.
- **Finishing-punch mix** (Barley 2025): hook 51%, straight 35%, uppercut/overhand 14%; longer fighters finish with straights, shorter with hooks/overhands.

### Where it conflicts / is missing
- Pressure vs counter styles: no direct comparison; only indirect (counters/combinations more effective for KOs, Pinto 2020; block-and-counter 2.8 vs 0.1, Davis 2013; counters ≈ 1/3 of initiations, Menescardi 2019).
- Small-n tactical studies (Coswig 2019 n = 15; Bhumipol 2023 n = 8; Folhes 2023 n = 20; Dunn 2017 19 bouts) give direction only.

> **Conclusions for the simulator**
> 1. (rule) Landing probabilities by punch: jab 0.28; cross/hooks 0.33–0.40; uppercuts rare; elite overall 0.28–0.33. Defence success: footwork 0.70; arm block/parry and trunk (slip/roll) ~0.5. Each skill tier adds ~+20% action rate on offence and defence.
> 2. (rule) "Counter after successful defence" is a distinct, skill-gated action with a high hit rate and extra KO weight; combinations (3-punch) and jab-cross carry extra win value; simultaneous counters are a first-class transition from a direct attack.
> 3. (rule) Exchange initiation mix ≈ 1/3 attack, 1/3 counter, 1/3 defensive/positioning. Winners' accuracy rises R1 → R3 via a conditioning/skill–accuracy coupling; losers' decays.
> 4. (rule) Aggression/output (HI time) is a strong round-scoring driver when accuracy is comparable; ground-and-pound and kick sequences discriminate MMA winners; body-strike attempts and speculative submission attempts have low or negative value.
> 5. (rule) Default kickboxing/MMA-striker mix: straights and roundhouse dominant; winners' profile adds hooks, combinations, clinch and footwork defence. Muay Thai: winners raise kick volume/accuracy in R5.
> 6. (rule) Era parameter: post-2008 distance-striking share higher than early grappling-dominant profiles; takedown density declining.

## 9. Character animation and physics-based characters

(Technical/industry sources; no clinical evidence. Target: TypeScript + React + Three.js r169, capsule humanoids, deterministic 10 Hz fixed-timestep engine, MIT repo, Intel Arc 140V iGPU, 60 fps "near-4K".)

### What the sources agree on
- **Motion matching (MM)** replaces hand-authored blend trees for locomotion: at runtime find the frame in an unstructured mocap DB best matching current pose (foot positions/velocities, hip velocity) and desired future trajectory (positions + facing at ~0.33/0.66/1.0 s), then blend/inertialize (Clavet GDC 2016: "5 or 10 minutes of a person running around"). Memory scales linearly with data; Learned MM (Holden 2020) replaces the DB with Decompressor/Stepper/Projector networks for large DBs — unnecessary for a minutes-scale fight DB. Holden's "simulation object vs character" split: a critically-damped-spring capsule carries gameplay position; the character follows via MM on the relative trajectory; drift corrected by clamping (e.g. `max_adjustment_ratio = 0.5`). Holden's reference implementation runs in the browser via emscripten; brute-force KD-tree search over ~20k frames at 10 Hz is < 1 ms in JS/WASM. **Code is MIT; bundled LAFAN1 data is CC BY-NC-ND (not redistributable).** DReCon uses MM as the front-end of a PD-tracked ragdoll (Bergamin 2019). For Honor used MM for locomotion with authored attacks.
- **Inertialization** (Bollo GDC 2018): at a transition record per-channel offset `x0 = old − new` and velocity `v0`, decay to zero with a quintic over `t1` (clamped to `−5·x0/v0` if velocity points away); only the new pose is evaluated (large CPU win vs dual-evaluation crossfades in Gears 4); quaternions as axis-angle offsets. Three.js `AnimationMixer` does classic dual-evaluation crossfades; additive clips via `AnimationUtils.makeClipAdditive`. The "Half Pound Filter" (arXiv 2602.21702, 2026) is a 1-Euro variant with automatic triggering on derivative discontinuities.
- **IK.** Two-bone analytic IK is exact, O(1), and what shipped engines use for limbs; FABRIK (Aristidou & Lasenby 2011) converges in few iterations for longer chains with constraints; CCD is simplest but curls joints; full-body IK is only needed for whole-body reach with balance. ozz-animation foot-IK: two-bone + aim IK, pelvis offset, raycast ground.
- **Physics-based characters.** Two families: RL tracking controllers (DeepMimic → AMP → ASE) taskable via discriminators; tracking-by-supervision (SuperTrack: world model + backprop, faster training, higher quality) and DReCon (low runtime cost). Two-player boxing yields emergent tactics (Won 2021; NCP/Zhu 2023 with VQ-VAE priors and emergent dodging; MAAIP/Younes 2023 boxing + full-body martial art), but these are research demos trained for days on GPUs with non-commercial datasets and are not deterministic across machines. Contact-rich two-character tracking (Zhang 2026, progressive experts) and diffusion-policy control (PDP 2024) are far from production. **Shipped games use driven ragdolls**: joint motors track the animation pose, impacts perturb it, blend weight controls physicality (EA Frostbite GDC; Respawn Jedi: Fallen Order GDC).
- **Hit reactions.** Commercial MMA/fighting games layer (1) authored directional reaction clips by strike type/zone/power, (2) additive procedural offsets (head snap, torso twist) scaled by impact, (3) full ragdoll only on knockdown/KO. UFC 5 "Real Impact" tracks **8 body regions × 5 damage levels** for visuals/HUD.
- **Two-person interaction.** State of the art is generative (InterGen/InterHuman IJCV 2024; Inter-X CVPR 2024 at 120 fps, 40 categories; Duolando ICLR 2024; DuetGen SIGGRAPH 2025; Ready-to-React ICLR 2025 — real-time reactive boxing policies; Interaction Mix and Match SCA 2022; Neural Animation Layering for martial arts SIGGRAPH 2021; multi-character retargeting SIGGRAPH 2023; game-tree fighting scenes). **Every dataset in this family is CC BY-NC-SA or stricter and the models inherit it.** Commercial clinch/ground practice: paired ("synced") clips on a shared interaction root with IK contact constraints and stance-based state machines.

> **Conclusions for the simulator**
> 1. (rule) All animation and physics are **cosmetic and downstream of the deterministic 10 Hz engine**; the render layer interpolates and embellishes; replays must be seed-replayable and never depend on animation.
> 2. (rule) Locomotion via MM in the render layer: 27-D Holden-style feature vector (L/R foot positions 6, foot velocities 6, hip velocity 3, future root positions at +0.33/+0.66/+1.0 s 6, future facing 6), normalised by DB std-dev, weights ≈ foot pos 0.75, foot vel 1.0, hip vel 1.0, traj pos 1.0, traj dir 1.5 (tune); search every engine tick (0.1 s) and on action-state change; keep playing if the best match is within ± 0.2 s of the current frame; DB ≈ 5–10 min of boxing/MMA footwork (orthodox + southpaw, shuffle, pivot, circle, cut-off, retreat, level change) fitted to one skeleton from ACCAD/CMU/Rokoko — never LAFAN1.
> 3. (rule) Strikes/takedowns are event-driven tagged clips with `windup/hit/recover` markup mapped 1:1 to engine action phases.
> 4. (rule) Own the pose pipeline: sample clips with `AnimationMixer`, blend in a Float32 pose buffer with inertialization — locomotion→locomotion 0.2–0.3 s; locomotion→strike 0.1–0.15 s; strike→hit-reaction 0.05–0.08 s; ground transitions 0.25 s.
> 5. (rule) ~60-line two-bone IK with pole vector for hip-knee-ankle and shoulder-elbow-wrist: foot planting with pelvis drop (`pelvis_y −= max(0, min(footL_drop, footR_drop))`, spring half-life 0.05 s); foot locking when clip foot speed < 0.15 m/s, release when foot travels > 5 cm, blend back over 0.1 s; hand targets for clinch/ground sockets (neck/hip/wrist); FABRIK for spine on fence leans; CCD only for prototyping.
> 6. (rule) Hit reactions: reaction matrix {zone head/body/leg} × {direction L/R/front} × {power light/medium/heavy/stagger}; additive critically-damped spring on head/upper-spine (ω ≈ 25 rad/s, angular velocity ∝ power, cap 35°); heavy → stagger clip + IK stumble; reaction latency 0–1 render frames, interpolating the contact instant from the action's `resolve` tick.
> 7. (rule) Knockdowns: replay-safe driven ragdoll in Rapier (WASM, fixed step) spawned at the engine `KD` event from the last kinematic pose and velocities; joint stiffness decays 1 → 0 over 0.4 s; strike impulse `J = strike_power_normalized × 60–120 N·s` at the contact bone; blend in with inertialization; record the resulting pose stream into the replay. Learned controllers only for an offline "hero replay" later.
> 8. (rule) Grappling: paired clips keyed by engine `groundPosition`/clinch states on an interaction root at the fighters' midpoint oriented along facing; per-fighter offsets scale with height; IK sockets hold contact; transition clips correspond exactly to engine transitions. ML two-person synthesis stays a research track.

## 10. Game AI for fighting/sports

### What the sources agree on
- **Killer Instinct Shadow AI** (Neal & Hayles GDC 2016): case-based reasoning — every recorded action stored with world-state context (health, meter, distance, time) as 400–700 patterns per match; retrieved with 40+ similarity metrics, ranked by value, occasionally picking a lower-ranked action for unpredictability; reaction time inherited from recorded human timing; strategy shifts when a pattern is repeatedly punished. Tekken 8 "Ghost" does this online.
- **Utility AI (IAUS; Mark & Lewis)** scores candidate actions as products of response-curve "considerations" over normalised inputs, picks the max (or weighted-random among top-k); data-driven, cheap, personality via per-fighter curve weights.
- **GOAP/HTN** (Orkin F.E.A.R.; Guerrilla Killzone 2/3 at ≈ 500 plans/s; Decima) suit multi-step squad plans; HTN Fighter (FightingICE, CIG 2017) works but must replan every frame. **MCTS** in FightingICE beats rule-based AI within a 16.67 ms budget when the forward model is cheap (Yoshida 2016); opponent-modelled action tables improve it; personas and dynamic difficulty via biased rollout rewards (CIG 2017; ESWA 2022); dynamic scripting with rule weights (Adaptive AI for Fighting Games).
- **Sports sims** (EA UFC GDC 2015; Madden/2K) drive CPU behaviour from tendency ratings plus stochastic outcome resolution — the same shape as this engine.

> **Conclusions for the simulator**
> 1. (rule) Three-layer AI for a 10 Hz deterministic engine with seeded RNG: (a) per-tick utility layer over the legal `actions` catalogue with considerations = range/stance, own and opponent stamina, per-zone damage, round/time, cage position, `lastStruckTick`, opponent's most likely next action; fighter style = weight vector (pressure, counter, wrestler, kickboxer); perception delay 1–2 ticks (100–200 ms; human simple RT 150–250 ms); (b) online opponent model: action-table counts `P(opp_action | range_bucket, opp_stamina_bucket)` with Laplace smoothing and exponential forgetting (τ ≈ 60 ticks), deterministic; (c) optional MCTS lookahead by cloning engine + RNG, 3–5 tick rollouts, N ≈ 64 playouts, only for big decisions (takedown, all-in flurry, clinch break), ≤ 2 ms in a worker; personas/difficulty via reward/utility shaping, never cheating inputs.
> 2. (rule) No behaviour trees; a tiny HTN-style macro (e.g. cut off cage → feint → level change) for multi-tick plans, replanned every tick.
> 3. (rule) Tie AI considerations to the evidence in Themes 6–8: prefer landed accuracy over volume; rocked opponents → sharply raised aggression; hurt experienced fighters bias to clinch/grab/shoot, inexperienced to trade (ESTIMATE behaviours from DAMAGE_PHYSIOLOGY).

## 11. Real-time rendering of humans (and broadcast cinematography)

### What the sources agree on
- **Skin** = diffuse with SSS + dual-lobe specular (two roughnesses e.g. 0.35/0.7, F0 ≈ 0.028) + micro-normal detail + thin water layer for sweat. Pre-integrated SSS (Penner & Borshukov 2011) needs no extra passes (2-D LUT over N·L × curvature plus a normal blur) — ideal for an iGPU and a forward renderer. Separable SSS (Jimenez 2015: two 1-D passes, 7 samples/px, < 0.5 ms on 2015 hardware) is the quality step-up but needs a masked screen-space pass (built into Babylon.js, custom in Three.js). Eyes (Jimenez GDC 2013): separate sclera/cornea, iris refraction, caustic, wet meniscus — close-ups only. Hair: strand grooms (UE5, UFC 5) are out of budget; hair cards/textures suffice, and MMA rosters are mostly short/shaved hair. UFC 5 uses a dual-lobe skin shader with GPU emitter-graph sweat/blood and strand hair; UFC 6 adds layer-based moisture/damage reflecting overhead lighting.
- **Hardware.** Arc 140V: 8 Xe2 cores @ up to 2.05 GHz; 3DMark Time Spy ≈ 3.6–4.2k; 1080p Cyberpunk medium ≈ 40–48 fps, FFXIV high ≈ 45–52 fps, Civ VI high ≈ 72 fps — roughly GTX 1050/1650 class. Native 3840 × 2160 at 60 fps with SSS + AO + TAA + bloom is not achievable in any engine on it; 2560 × 1440 internal + temporal upscale is the realistic "near-4K". XeGTAO measured 2.39 ms at 1080p high on an Iris Xe (i7-1195G7), ~0.56 ms on an RTX 2060; GTAO ≈ 0.5 ms on console (Jimenez 2016).
- **Three.js state.** r169 is WebGL2 with legacy `EffectComposer` (GTAO, SMAA, bloom, thickness-map SSS example exist); the modern post stack (TRAA, SSGI, SSR, DoF, motion blur, AO node, `MeshSSSNodeMaterial`, TSL) only exists on `WebGPURenderer` in r17x+, with WebGL2 fallback (`webgpu_postprocessing_sss` is screen-space *shadows*, not subsurface). WebGPU ships by default in Chrome/Edge 113+, Firefox 141+ (Windows), Safari 26.
- **Broadcast camera language.** UFC Fight Island: 6 Octagon cameras (4 manned handheld HDC-4300 + 2 Talon robotic heads, one overhead on the lighting truss, one outside), RF Steadicam, 24-ft jib at 6 o'clock, 2 cameras in 6 × super-slow-motion, 2 apron robotic corner POVs; cage-side Alexa Mini handheld 19–90 mm ~f/4; promo MoVI 35 mm f/2.8–4; operators on raised platforms. HBO boxing: two tripod "hard" cameras covering both fighters (head-to-toe and head-to-waist), a 90° camera for far corners, two apron handhelds, robotic, super-slow-mo, JitaCam/SkyCam. Coverage is dominated by one hard wide "game camera", a tighter second hard camera, cage-side handhelds for clinch/fence work and corners, overhead robotic for ground fighting, jib for walkouts/wides/tale-of-the-tape, 6 × slow-mo replays of knockdowns from the nearest handheld then a second angle. UFC 5's replay director is event-aware (strike type, limb, direction choose placement; slow-mo ramps and DoF only in replays; doctor-stoppage close-ups on six injury zones). Academic camera control formalises this as idioms/state machines (He 1996; Christie 2008); learning-based methods (Jiang 2020/2021; DanceCamera3D) can extract style from footage later.

> **Conclusions for the simulator**
> 1. (rule) Stay on Three.js; migrate r169 → r17x `WebGPURenderer` + TSL with WebGL2 fallback; author the skin material in TSL. No Unity/Unreal: their extra fidelity (Burley SSS, grooms, Lumen) is unaffordable on an Arc 140V at 60 fps.
> 2. (rule) Performance contract: 1440p internal (1080p on battery), TAA upscale to panel, 60 fps; geometry + shadows ≤ 5 ms (2 fighters ~60k tris each, one 2048² cascaded/PCF shadow map, crowd as impostor cards); post ≤ 4 ms (GTAO ≈ 1.5–2.5 ms at 1440p on Arc 140V, TAA/TRAA 1 ms, bloom mip chain threshold 1.0 strength 0.15 ≈ 0.8 ms, ACES (r169) → AgX (r17x), subtle vignette + grain); DoF/motion blur only in replays at 30 fps. Test on the Arc 140V laptop.
> 3. (rule) Skin: pre-integrated LUT SSS + dual-lobe specular in a custom `ShaderMaterial` now, TSL node material later; sweat as `clearcoat` 0 → 1 ramped by `fightClock/900 s` and per-zone exertion, `clearcoatRoughness` 0.05–0.15, albedo wet-darkening × 0.85, scrolling droplet normal map; blood decals via an 8-zone damage mask synchronised with engine damage; short hair only.
> 4. (rule) Lighting: 4–6 overhead spot lights (~5600 K key ring at 6–8 m, 45–60° down), cool arena rim, dim warm canvas bounce, HDR env map of the arena for sweat reflections.
> 5. (rule) `BroadcastDirector` shot-idiom state machine in `src/render/cameras.ts`: `HARD_WIDE` (default; 35 mm-equiv, 6 o'clock ~7 m out, 1.6 m high, 55° FOV, damped pan/zoom, 60% safe frame); `HARD_TIGHT` (85 mm-equiv, head-to-waist); `CAGESIDE_HANDHELD` (1.2 m high, 2.5–3.5 m from midpoint, perpendicular, ± 2° Perlin shake at 1–2 Hz, lens breathing); `OVERHEAD` (6.5 m up, 20° off-vertical) for ground; `JIB` (10–15 s arc) for walkouts/breaks; `CORNER_POV` between rounds. Minimum shot 4 s live / 2 s replay; cut to `HARD_TIGHT` after 8 s of low activity; `CAGESIDE_HANDHELD` on clinch/fence pin; `OVERHEAD` on takedown completion; back to `HARD_WIDE` on stand-up; **never cut within ± 0.4 s of a strike resolve**. Replays on `KD`, `KO`, `TKO` and strikes with `value ≥ 0.8`: (1) nearest handheld at 0.25 × from −1.5 s to +1.5 s with f/2.8 DoF, (2) overhead/opposite side at 0.5 ×; queued to the next natural pause. HUD: tale-of-the-tape, round/clock/strike chyron, 8-zone damage icons, significant-strike ticker from the engine log.

## 14. Weak or conflicting evidence — items the sim must mark as ASSUMPTION

1. **Leg kicks and body shots.** No quantitative dose-response for accumulated leg-kick or body-shot effects on mobility, guard, cardio or finish probability (only Razm 2026 narrative). Leg-damage → mobility curve, calf-kick "foot drop" (5%/clean landing, 30–90 s), checked-kick damage split (60/15%), catastrophic shin fracture 0.3%, leg-kick TKO ~1–2% of finishes, liver-shot delayed collapse (0.5–3 s delay, 5–20 s down), solar-plexus 3–15 s, body-finish share 4–6% — all ESTIMATE.
2. **Flash knockdown vs accumulated damage** is not separated anywhere; the only proxies are Hutchison's 2.6 extra strikes / 3.5 s and Miele & Bailes' fatal-bout punch counts. Rocked/stunned/KD state durations, thresholds (45/65/90), decay half-lives (8/12/20 s), structural decay (25%/15%/10% per break), permanent fraction (40%), progressive-vulnerability slope (0.006), kKO = 0.22 and the 45/25/8 split, knockdown → finish conversion 55–65% — ESTIMATE.
3. **Fatigue → striking force.** Lab decrements (Dunn 2022; Ouergui 2013) vs no in-ring decline (Pierce 2006, n = 4 by round) and rising winner accuracy (Davis 2018). Treat force loss as ≤ 10% and put fatigue into volume/accuracy/defence. Per-action energy costs, clearance constants, "second wind", "adrenaline dump", HR recovery values — ESTIMATE.
4. **Round-by-round MMA pacing** is inconsistent (del Vecchio vs Miarka 2016 vs Antoniettô 2023; female rounds stationary). Use flat base rates with state modulation.
5. **Weight class and per-strike force.** Lab: heavier = harder via effective mass; in-ring landed force uncorrelated with mass (r = 0.22); yet KO rate rises ~4–7 × FLW → HW. Weight should enter via KO susceptibility/effective-mass tail and attacker-mass dominance, not mean force; `bodyMassScale` exponent −0.5 is ESTIMATE.
6. **Concussion thresholds** are all from football or dummies (Zhang 2004 [S-mem]; Rowson 2012/2013); the combat-specific concussive vs non-concussive RA means (Lota 2022) are small-n. Boxing pulses are shorter and more rotational, so HIC under-predicts and β2 may need × 1.3–1.5. Head-kick and knee RA have never been measured; the 1.5–2 × hook scaling from a 130 g helmeted linear figure (Fife 2013) is ESTIMATE. Lever multipliers (1.3/1.2/0.7/0.8), unseen × 1.35, mouth-open × 1.2, chin attribute ± 1450 rad/s², dehydration `z += 3d`, ground × 0.7 RA / × 1.3 structural — ESTIMATE.
7. **Clinch as a phase** is almost never coded in MMA time-motion (only Miarka 2017's 10–13 s clinch-without-attack); clinch durations are borrowed from wrestling (37 s) and judo (24–29 s).
8. **Referee / doctor / corner stoppage** has no formal model — only descriptive counts (Hutchison 2014; Miarka 2022; κ 0.86 for coding). Thresholds, doctor-stoppage share 2–4%, corner retirement 1–2%, eye-poke rate 0.6%, point-deduction behaviour — ESTIMATE; foul recovery "up to 5 min" and Zhang 2004 thresholds are [S-mem].
9. **Cut rate by weapon.** No study separates elbows from punches; the 5 × elbow multiplier (6% vs 1.2%/0.4%), knee 3%, head clash 8%, severity model, cutman effect, vision penalties — ESTIMATE anchored only on laceration shares (36.7–59.4% MMA; 51% / 47.9% boxing/MMA).
10. **Simulated vs official bouts.** Nearly all MMA physiology is sparring with n = 8–20; official karate bouts ran ~40% more lactate; treat simulated numbers as under-estimates.
11. **Effective mass by punch type** (straights > hooks; elite leg drive 39% vs 16%) came from a search summary of MDPI Applied Sciences 2025 15(7):4008 (doi:10.3390/app15074008) — page not fetched; unverified.
12. **Head-contact counts from sensors** disagree 2–3 × by device (Le Flao 2024); use video-coded rates.
13. **Weight regain vs outcome.** Positive in commission datasets (Faro OR 1.046; Baribeau +7%/+13%), null in elite samples (Kirk 2020 d = 0.23; Peacock p = 0.80); none control for skill. Small capped effect; dehydration-to-chin mechanism unquantified in humans; typical 5–10% cut is ESTIMATE (Crighton 2016 title only).
14. **Reach advantage.** Null in MMA (four Kirk datasets); positive only at heavyweight, in uncapped HW boxing (Fiala, abstract-level) and in Collier's fight-level probit (book chapter). Popular "reach-advantaged fighter wins 51.65% / −7 cm wins 37%" figures are non-peer-reviewed and excluded.
15. **Southpaw advantage.** Over-representation robust; edge small in records-based samples (P-sup 52–55%) and null in matched fights; online "10–15% more title wins" untraceable. Per-fight bonus ≤ 2% and its decay with exposure are design choices.
16. **Age curve.** Only direction (r = 0.18) and late-career output decline are peer-reviewed; "peak 27–32" and "career-year-4 64% win peak" are non-peer-reviewed. Age → kKO slopes (+4%/yr > 30, +8%/yr > 35) ESTIMATE.
17. **Beginner behaviours** (flinch, eye closure, turning away, squaring up): no combat-specific quantification; only Ottoboni 2015, Williams & Elliott 1999 and startle literature. Read probabilities by tier, counter-on-read 0.05/0.25/0.5, feint-bite 0.6/0.25, anxiety penalties −15%/−5%, 50–100 ms cue advantage — proposals.
18. **Pressure vs counter styles.** No peer-reviewed head-to-head in MMA/boxing; indirect only (Pinto 2020; Davis 2013; Menescardi 2019).
19. **Judging weights.** AME vector from an examined PhD thesis (Holmes 2022); Gift 2018 and Collier 2012 agree on ordering but coefficients inaccessible; "visible damage ≈ 0.3–0.5 KD" and previous-round/favourite bonuses +3–5% are proposals. Collier's height effect contradicts Kirk (low weight).
20. **ML accuracy claims** above the 62–67% peer-reviewed band (70–80% in course projects; withdrawn FightTracker arXiv 2312.11067) were excluded.
21. **Small-n tactical studies** (Coswig 2019 n = 15; Bhumipol 2023 n = 8; Folhes 2023 n = 20; Dunn 2017 19 bouts; Liu 2022 8 vs 8; Loturco 2021 n = 8) give direction and rough magnitude only. Ma 2025 (The Sport Journal) is low-tier with survivorship bias — use only for method-of-victory proportions, cross-checked with Pinto 2020.
22. **Submission timing.** No direct tap-latency measurement or injury-per-refused-tap dataset; "tap 3–7 s after full lock", "refused tap → fracture in 1–3 s", "consciousness back 2–5 s" are [EST]; Reay & Holloway 1982, Kochhar's Newton values and full Piekarski/Hasegawa PDFs were not retrievable; several submission-appendix items (Rossen 1943, Strangulation Training Institute timeline, Demartini 2017, Martinet 2025, Kreiswirth 2014, Stellpflug 2024/2025, pressure values 3.4 vs 22 N/cm²) have no DOI/URL in the source and are excluded from the bibliography. The Fares 2026 serious-injury denominator differs between inputs (7/97 vs ~7/80).
23. **Attribution discrepancy.** DAMAGE_PHYSIOLOGY cites "Ouergui et al. 2017, IJSPP, PMID 27197115" for the low-kick kickboxing E:P numbers that LIT_A attributes to Slimani, Chaabene, Miarka, Chamari 2017 IJSPP 12(2):182–189; treated as the same paper pending verification.
24. **Asset licences.** CMU licence wording is snippet-only (TLS failure); Rokoko has no formal licence text; Mixamo FAQ returned 403 (terms via third-party summary); SMPL-X licence page 404. Re-verify before shipping.
25. **Animation/rendering/AI parameters** (blend times, IK thresholds, spring constants, impulse magnitudes, camera geometry, frame budgets scaled from Iris Xe to Arc 140V) are industry-derived design choices, not measurements; learned-controller and two-person-synthesis results are non-deterministic and licence-incompatible.
26. **Grey literature not fetched**: UFC Performance Institute reports (2018, 2021), Jack Slack essays, Fightnomics, ARP individual position statements (index only), Unified Rules (Wikipedia summary) — best places to calibrate ESTIMATEs but not usable as sources yet.
